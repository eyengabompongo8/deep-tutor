## Module B1.1: Imparfait vs Passé Composé

### Topic ID: B1.1_IMPARFAIT_FORMATION
- **Title:** Formation & Stem Rules of the Imperfect Tense (l'Imparfait)
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Master the morphological formation of l'imparfait by deriving stems from the 1st person plural (*nous*) present tense form and applying the standard endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient).
- **Prerequisites:** A1.2_PRESENT_TENSE_REGULAR, A2.1_VERB_CONJUGATION
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/imperfect-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Base stem derivation: Take the 1st person plural (*nous*) present tense form of any verb and drop the *-ons* suffix (e.g., *parlons* -> *parl-*, *finissons* -> *finiss-*, *vendons* -> *vend-*).
  2. Imparfait endings across all three conjugation groups (-er, -ir, -re) and irregulars: *je -ais*, *tu -ais*, *il/elle/on -ait*, *nous -ions*, *vous -iez*, *ils/elles -aient*.
  3. Irregular stem exception: The verb *être* is the sole irregular stem exception (*ét-*): *j'étais*, *tu étais*, *il était*, *nous étions*, *vous étiez*, *ils étaient*.
  4. Orthographic adjustments: Verbs ending in *-cer* take a cedilla (*ç*) before *-a* (*je plaçais*), and verbs ending in *-ger* retain an *e* before *-a* (*je mangeais*), but revert to standard spelling before *i* (*nous placions*, *nous mangions*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *autrefois* (formerly)
  - *chaque jour / chaque année* (every day / every year)
  - *toujours / fréquemment* (always / frequently)
  - *en ce temps-là* (in those days)
  - *d'habitude / habituellement* (usually)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Quand j'étais jeune, nous habitions à la campagne. | Cuando era joven, vivíamos en el campo. / When I was young, we lived in the countryside. | Imparfait stem (*ét-*, *habit-*) + endings (*-ais*, *-ions*) |
| Chaque été, ils finissaient leurs devoirs avant de jouer. | Cada verano, terminaban sus deberes antes de jugar. / Every summer, they finished their homework before playing. | 2nd group verb imparfait stem (*finiss-*) + ending (*-aient*) |
| Nous mangions des croissants tous les matins. | Comíamos croissants todas las mañanas. / We ate croissants every morning. | Orthographic adjustments in *-ger* verb (*mangions*) |

- **Linguistic Criteria:** POS = `VERB` or `AUX`, Morph contains `Aspect=Imp` or `Tense=Imp`, `VerbForm=Fin`.

```python
    [{"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}, "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["Tense=Imp"]}}],
```

### Topic ID: B1.1_IMPARFAIT_USES
- **Title:** Aspectual Uses of the Imparfait: Habitual Actions, Descriptions, & Background States
- **Category:** Grammar
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Apply l'imparfait appropriately to convey uncompleted past actions, habitual/iterative past routines, physical and emotional descriptions, weather conditions, time, and background contexts.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.1_IMPARFAIT_FORMATION
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/imperfect-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Habitual past actions: Used for actions repeated regularly over an indefinite period without explicit temporal endpoints (*Il se promenait tous les soirs*).
  2. Physical, mental, and emotional states: Used for conditions, feelings, intentions, and desires in the past (*Elle avait peur*, *Il voulait partir*).
  3. Descriptive backgrounds: Establishes setting, weather, scenery, age, and time in past narratives (*Il faisait beau*, *Le soleil brillait*, *J'avais dix ans*).
  4. Ongoing uncompleted processes: Represents an action in progress in the past ("was doing"), equivalent to *être en train de* in the imperfect (*Il lisait pendant le voyage*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *d'habitude* (usually)
  - *tous les jours / toutes les semaines* (every day / every week)
  - *souvent / parfois / rarement* (often / sometimes / rarely)
  - *à l'époque* (at that time)
  - *pendant que* (while)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il faisait très froid et la neige tombait sans arrêt. | Hacía mucho frío y la nieve caía sin parar. / It was very cold and the snow was falling continuously. | Weather description & continuous action (*faisait*, *tombait*) |
| Elle allait au cinéma tous les samedis avec ses amis. | Ella iba al cine todos los sábados con sus amigos. / She used to go to the cinema every Saturday with her friends. | Past habit/routine (*allait* + frequency marker) |
| Nous avions vingt ans et nous voulions changer le monde. | Teníamos veinte años y queríamos cambiar el mundo. / We were twenty years old and wanted to change the world. | Age & mental/emotional state (*avions*, *voulions*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Imparfait verb clause co-occurring with habitual adverbs or descriptive context clauses.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    {"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}, "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["Tense=Imp"]}}
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Disambiguate *pendant que* clauses containing imparfait verbs from *pendant* + bounded duration noun phrases triggering passé composé.

### Topic ID: B1.1_PASSE_COMPOSE_VS_IMPARFAIT
- **Title:** Passé Composé vs Imparfait Contrast & Interrupted Actions
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Contrast completed specific events (Passé Composé) with background continuous states or ongoing actions (Imparfait) within single and compound complex sentences.
- **Prerequisites:** A2.1_PASSE_COMPOSE, B1.1_IMPARFAIT_USES
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/passe-compose-vs-imparfait/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Aspectual opposition: Imparfait sets the background scene (ongoing, state, habit, process), while Passé Composé expresses bounded, punctual, completed events that advance the narrative timeline.
  2. Interrupted actions rule: In complex sentences joined by *quand* or *lorsque*, the ongoing background action takes l'imparfait while the sudden interrupting action takes le passé composé (*Je dormais quand le téléphone a sonné*).
  3. Concurrent ongoing actions: When two past actions occurred simultaneously as ongoing background processes, both take l'imparfait, connected by *pendant que* (*Pendant qu'il cuisinait, elle lisait*).
  4. Sequential vs continuous events: Sequential completed actions use series of Passé Composé verbs; background descriptions surrounding them use Imparfait.

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *quand / lorsque* (when)
  - *soudain / tout à coup* (suddenly)
  - *pendant que* (while)
  - *une fois / un jour* (once / one day)
  - *ce jour-là* (that day)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Je dormais paisiblement quand le téléphone a sonné. | Dormía plácidamente cuando el teléfono sonó. / I was sleeping peacefully when the phone rang. | Imparfait background (*dormais*) interrupted by Passé Composé (*a sonné*) |
| Pendant que Marie révisait, Paul a préparé le dîner. | Mientras Marie repasaba, Paul preparó la cena. / While Marie was studying, Paul prepared dinner. | Continuous background (*révisait*) + completed event (*a préparé*) |
| Hier à huit heures, il pleuvait, alors nous sommes restés à la maison. | Ayer a las ocho llovía, así que nos quedamos en casa. / Yesterday at eight o'clock, it was raining, so we stayed home. | Weather state (*pleuvait*) leading to completed decision (*sommes restés*) |

### Topic ID: B1.1_TEMPORAL_MARKERS_PAST
- **Title:** Past Temporal Expressions & Duration Markers (Depuis, Pendant, Il y a, En)
- **Category:** Vocabulary
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Distinguish and correctly deploy past time prepositions (*depuis*, *pendant*, *il y a*, *en*, *dans*) to anchor past actions, duration, and elapsed time relative to the moment of speech.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.1_PASSE_COMPOSE_VS_IMPARFAIT
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/imperfect-tense/, https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/prepositions/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. *Il y a* + duration + past tense: Indicates a completed action in the past, measuring time elapsed since the event occurred ("ago"). Takes Passé Composé (*Il est parti il y a trois jours*).
  2. *Pendant* + duration: Expresses a completed duration with a defined beginning and end. Used with Passé Composé for bounded past events (*J'ai habité à Paris pendant cinq ans*).
  3. *Depuis* + time expression: Expresses an action that started in the past and continues into the present (takes Present tense: *J'habite ici depuis trois ans*) or started in the remote past and continued up to another past point (takes Imparfait: *J'habitais là depuis un an quand...*).
  4. *En* + duration: Expresses the amount of time required to perform/complete an action (*Il a terminé le projet en deux heures*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *il y a* + [time duration] (ago)
  - *pendant* + [bounded duration] (for / during)
  - *depuis* + [starting point / ongoing duration] (since / for)
  - *en* + [amount of time required] (in / within)
  - *dans* + [future duration relative to present] (in)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Mon frère a obtenu son diplôme il y a deux ans. | Mi hermano se graduó hace dos años. / My brother graduated two years ago. | *Il y a* + duration marker with Passé Composé |
| Elle a étudié l'espagnol pendant trois ans au lycée. | Ella estudió español durante tres años en el instituto. / She studied Spanish for three years in high school. | *Pendant* + bounded time duration |
| J'habitais en France depuis six mois quand j'ai rencontré Marc. | Vivía en Francia desde hacía seis meses cuando conocí a Marc. / I had been living in France for six months when I met Marc. | *Depuis* + duration combined with Imparfait & Passé Composé |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Temporal prepositions (*il y a*, *pendant*, *depuis*, *en*) combined with numerical time duration noun phrases.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    [{"LEMMA": {"IN": ["depuis", "pendant"]}}],
    [{"LEMMA": "en"}, {"POS": "NUM"}],
    [{"LEMMA": "il"}, {"LEMMA": "y"}, {"LEMMA": "avoir"}],
    [{"LEMMA": "pour"}, {"POS": "NUM"}]
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Distinguish *en* as a temporal duration marker (*en deux heures*) from *en* as a spatial preposition (*en France*) by checking noun head semantic category.

## Module B1.2: Future & Conditional Tenses

### Topic ID: B1.2_FUTUR_SIMPLE
- **Title:** Formation & Syntax of the Futur Simple
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Form and apply the simple future tense across regular and irregular verbs for predictions, formal commitments, plans, and conditional main clauses.
- **Prerequisites:** A1.2_VERBS, A2.2_FUTUR_PROCHE
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/future-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Base stem for regular verbs: Use the full infinitive for *-er* and *-ir* verbs (e.g., *parler-*, *finir-*), and drop the final *-e* for *-re* verbs (e.g., *vendr-*).
  2. Future endings (identical for ALL verbs derived from present conjugation of *avoir*): *-ai*, *-as*, *-a*, *-ons*, *-ez*, *-ont*.
  3. Key irregular stems: *avoir* -> *aur-*, *être* -> *ser-*, *aller* -> *ir-*, *faire* -> *fer-*, *pouvoir* -> *pourr-*, *vouloir* -> *voudr-*, *devoir* -> *devr-*, *venir* -> *viendr-*, *voir* -> *verr-*, *savoir* -> *saur-*, *falloir* -> *faudr-*.
  4. Syntactic usage: Expresses actions taking place further in the future or formal/written commitments, distinct from immediate plans (*futur proche* with *aller* + infinitive).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *demain* (tomorrow)
  - *la semaine prochaine / l'année prochaine* (next week / next year)
  - *bientôt / plus tard* (soon / later)
  - *dans un mois* (in a month)
  - *à l'avenir* (in the future)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| L'année prochaine, nous voyagerons en Italie. | El año que viene viajaremos a Italia. / Next year, we will travel to Italy. | Regular *-er* verb future stem (*voyager-*) + ending (*-ons*) |
| Quand tu arriveras à la gare, je serai là. | Cuando llegues a la estación, estaré allí. / When you arrive at the station, I will be there. | Future temporal clause (*arriveras*) + irregular stem (*serai*) |
| Ils pourront finir le rapport avant la fin de la semaine. | Podrán terminar el informe antes del final de la semana. / They will be able to finish the report before the end of the week. | Irregular stem verb (*pourron-t*) + infinitive complementary |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** POS = `VERB` or `AUX`, Morph contains `Tense=Fut`, `VerbForm=Fin`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    [{"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}, "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["Tense=Fut"]}}],
    [{"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}, "LEMMA": {"IN": ["voyager", "arriver", "aller", "faire", "prendre", "être", "avoir", "devoir", "savoir", "venir"]}}]
```

### Topic ID: B1.2_CONDITIONNEL_PRESENT
- **Title:** Conditionnel Présent: Polite Requests, Desires, & Potentialities
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Construct and utilize the present conditional by combining future stems with imperfect endings to communicate politeness, wishes, advice, and unconfirmed information.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.1_IMPARFAIT_FORMATION, B1.2_FUTUR_SIMPLE
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/conditional-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Morphological composition: Future tense stem + Imparfait tense endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient).
  2. Modality of politeness: Used with modal verbs (*vouloir*, *pouvoir*, *aimer*) to soften requests and demands (*Je voudrais un café*, *Pourriez-vous m'aider ?*).
  3. Giving advice: Used with *devoir* or *pouvoir* to formulate recommendations (*Tu devrais faire de l'exercice*).
  4. Unconfirmed information / hearsay: Used in journalistic contexts or reporting to present unverified news (*Le président arriverait ce soir*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *s'il vous plaît / s'il te plaît* (please)
  - *aimerais / voudrais* (would like)
  - *pourriez-vous* (could you)
  - *à votre place* (in your position)
  - *selon les rumeurs* (according to rumors)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Je voudrais réserver une table pour deux personnes. | Me gustaría reservar una mesa para dos personas. / I would like to reserve a table for two people. | Polite request modal conditional (*voudrais*) |
| Tu devrais consulter un médecin sans attendre. | Deberías consultar a un médico sin esperar. / You should consult a doctor without delay. | Conditional advice marker (*devrais*) |
| Selon le journal, l'entreprise recruterait cent employés. | Selon le journal, la entreprise contrataría a cien empleados. / According to the newspaper, the company would be hiring one hundred employees. | Journalistic unconfirmed information conditional (*recruterait*) |

### Topic ID: B1.2_SI_CLAUSES_HYPOTHESIS
- **Title:** Hypothetical Constructions with Si Clauses (Real & Unreal Conditionals)
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Formulate Type 1 (open/real conditions) and Type 2 (unreal/present hypothetical conditions) complex sentences governed by the conjunction *si*.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.1_IMPARFAIT_USES, B1.2_FUTUR_SIMPLE, B1.2_CONDITIONNEL_PRESENT
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/conditional-tense/, https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/future-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Type 1 Conditional (Real / Probable): *Si* + Present Indicative -> Future Simple (or Present Indicative / Imperative) in the result clause (*Si tu étudies, tu réussiras*).
  2. Type 2 Conditional (Unreal / Contrary-to-fact Present): *Si* + Imparfait -> Conditionnel Présent in the result clause (*Si j'avais de l'argent, j'acheterais une voiture*).
  3. Absolute restriction: *Si* CANNOT be followed directly by Futur Simple or Conditionnel within the hypothetical *si*-clause (*Si j'aurais* -> STRICT GRAMMATICAL ERROR; must be *Si j'avais*).
  4. Clause order flexibility: The *si*-clause can precede or follow the result clause without altering tense agreement rules (*J'irais au parc s'il faisait beau*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *si* (if)
  - *si seulement* (if only)
  - *même si* (even if)
  - *au cas où* (in case + conditionnel)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Si tu viens demain, nous irons visiter le musée. | Si vienes mañana, iremos a visitar el museo. / If you come tomorrow, we will go visit the museum. | Type 1: *Si* + Présent (*viens*) -> Futur Simple (*irons*) |
| Si nous avions plus de temps, nous apprendrions le japonais. | Si tuviéramos más tiempo, aprenderíamos japonés. / If we had more time, we would learn Japanese. | Type 2: *Si* + Imparfait (*avions*) -> Conditionnel Présent (*apprendrions*) |
| Je voyagerais autour du monde si je gagnais à la loterie. | Viajaría alrededor del mundo si ganara la lotería. / I would travel around the world if I won the lottery. | Inverted Type 2: Conditionnel result clause + *si* + Imparfait |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Conjunction *si* introducing a clause with Imparfait or Present verb, co-occurring with a main clause containing Futur Simple or Conditionnel Présent.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    [{"LEMMA": "si"}, {"OP": "*"}, {"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}}],
    [{"POS": {"IN": ["VERB", "AUX"]}, "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["Mood=Cnd"]}}, {"OP": "*"}, {"LEMMA": "si"}]
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Disambiguate indirect interrogative *si* ("I wonder if he will come" -> *Je me demande s'il viendra*) which permits future simple, from hypothetical *si* clauses.

### Topic ID: B1.2_FUTUR_ANTERIEUR
- **Title:** Futur Antérieur & Temporal Conjunctions (Quand, Lorsque, Dès que)
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Construct the compound future anterior tense (auxiliary in Futur Simple + Past Participle) to express actions that will be completed prior to another future action.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.2_FUTUR_SIMPLE, A2.1_PASSE_COMPOSE
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/future-tense/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Compound structure: Auxiliary verb *avoir* or *être* conjugated in the Futur Simple + Past Participle (*participe passé*) of the main verb (*j'aurai parlé*, *je serai parti*).
  2. Auxiliary selection & agreement: Follows identical auxiliary selection (*être* for house of *être* verbs and reflexive verbs) and past participle agreement rules as the Passé Composé.
  3. Temporal priority rule: In sentences with two future events linked by temporal conjunctions (*quand*, *lorsque*, *dès que*, *aussitôt que*, *une fois que*), the chronologically prior action MUST take Futur Antérieur, while the subsequent action takes Futur Simple (*Quand tu auras fini, nous partirons*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *quand* (when)
  - *lorsque* (when)
  - *dès que* (as soon as)
  - *aussitôt que* (as soon as)
  - *une fois que* (once)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Dès que vous aurez reçu le courriel, vous me préviendrez. | Tan pronto como hayan recibido el correo, me avisarán. / As soon as you have received the email, you will let me know. | Temporal conjunction (*Dès que*) + Futur Antérieur (*aurez reçu*) |
| Quand nous serons arrivés à l'hôtel, nous me reposerons. | Cuando hayamos llegado al hotel, descansaremos. / When we have arrived at the hotel, we will rest. | Auxiliary *être* future anterior (*serons arrivés*) + participle agreement |
| Une fois qu'elle aura terminé son discours, les questions commenceront. | Una vez que ella haya terminado su discurso, comenzarán las preguntas. / Once she has finished her speech, questions will begin. | Priority future action (*aura terminé*) before future simple result |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Conjunction (*dès que*, *quand*, *lorsque*) followed by future auxiliary (*aurai*, *seras*, etc.) and past participle.

```python
    [{"LEMMA": {"IN": ["dès", "aussitôt", "quand", "lorsque"]}}, {"OP": "*"}, {"POS": "AUX"}],

```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Verify that multi-word temporal markers like *dès que* are matched as single functional units before evaluating auxiliary dependencies.

## Module B1.3: Pronouns & Prepositional Structures

### Topic ID: B1.3_DIRECT_INDIRECT_OBJECT_PRONOUNS
- **Title:** Direct (COD) and Indirect (COI) Object Pronouns
- **Category:** Pronouns
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Identify, substitute, and correctly position direct object pronouns (*me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les*) and indirect object pronouns (*lui, leur*) in simple and compound tenses.
- **Prerequisites:** A2.3_PRONOUNS
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/object-pronouns/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Direct Object Pronouns (COD - Complement d'Objet Direct): Replace nouns directly acted upon without a preposition: *me (m')*, *te (t')*, *le (l')*, *la (l')*, *nous*, *vous*, *les*.
  2. Indirect Object Pronouns (COI - Complement d'Objet Indirect): Replace animate nouns preceded by the preposition *à* (verbs of communication/transfer): *me*, *te*, *lui* (masc/fem singular), *nous*, *vous*, *leur* (masc/fem plural). Note: *lui* and *leur* do NOT vary by gender.
  3. Pre-verbal placement: Object pronouns precede the finite verb in simple tenses (*Je le vois*), precede the auxiliary in compound tenses (*Je l'ai vu*), and precede the infinitive in dual-verb structures (*Je vais le voir*).
  4. Past participle agreement rule with COD: In compound tenses with *avoir*, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the preceding COD pronoun (*La lettre ? Je l'ai écrite*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - Direct object verbs: *voir*, *regarder*, *écouter*, *inviter*, *connaître*, *prendre*
  - Indirect object verbs (verbes à + personne): *parler à*, *téléphoner à*, *écrire à*, *dire à*, *donner à*, *demander à*

| Pierre téléphone à ses parents tous les dimanches ; il leur parle longtemps. | Pierre llama a sus padres todos los domingos; les habla mucho tiempo. / Pierre calls his parents every Sunday; he speaks to them for a long time. | COI pronoun (*leur*) replacing *à ses parents* |

### Topic ID: B1.3_ADVERBIAL_PRONOUNS_Y_EN
- **Title:** Adverbial Pronouns Y (Location/À + Noun) and EN (Quantity/De + Noun)
- **Category:** Pronouns
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Substitute prepositional phrases of location/destination (*à, en, dans, sur* + place) with *y*, and expressions of quantity/indefinite items (*de, du, de la, des* + noun) with *en*.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.3_DIRECT_INDIRECT_OBJECT_PRONOUNS
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/object-pronouns/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Adverbial Pronoun *Y*: Replaces spatial locations / destinations introduced by *à*, *en*, *dans*, *sur*, *chez* (*Il va à Paris -> Il y va*), and inanimate indirect objects introduced by *à* (*Je pense à mon avenir -> J'y pense*).
  2. Adverbial Pronoun *EN*: Replaces nouns preceded by partitive articles (*du, de la, des*), indefinite articles, or prepositions of origin *de* (*Tu veux du pain ? -> J'en veux*).
  3. Retention of numbers/quantifiers with *EN*: When *en* replaces a noun modified by a number or quantity expression, the number/quantifier must be retained at the end of the clause (*J'ai deux chiens -> J'en ai deux*).
  4. Placement: Positioned immediately before the conjugated verb or auxiliary (*J'y suis allé*, *Elle en a acheté*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *à / dans / en / sur / chez* + place (triggers *y*)
  - *penser à / réfléchir à* + inanimate (triggers *y*)
  - *du / de la / des / de* + noun (triggers *en*)
  - Quantifiers: *beaucoup de*, *un peu de*, *plusieurs*, numbers (triggers *en*)

| Avez-vous des pommes ? Oui, nous en avons trois dans le panier. | ¿Tienen manzanas? Sí, tenemos tres en la cesta. / Do you have apples? Yes, we have three of them in the basket. | Pronoun *en* replacing quantity with number retention (*trois*) |
| Elle pense souvent à son projet et elle y consacre tout son temps. | Ella piensa a menudo en su proyecto y le dedica todo su tiempo. / She often thinks about her project and devotes all her time to it. | Pronoun *y* replacing inanimate *à* complement (*à son projet*) |

### Topic ID: B1.3_DOUBLE_PRONOUN_PLACEMENT
- **Title:** Double Object Pronoun Order & Sentence Syntax
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Apply the multi-pronoun ordering chart across declarative, negative, and imperative clause structures containing multiple object pronouns.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.3_DIRECT_INDIRECT_OBJECT_PRONOUNS, B1.3_ADVERBIAL_PRONOUNS_Y_EN
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/object-pronouns/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Pre-verbal ordering hierarchy (Declarative & Negative clauses):
     - Block 1: *me / te / se / nous / vous*
     - Block 2: *le / la / les*
     - Block 3: *lui / leur*
     - Block 4: *y*
     - Block 5: *en*
     *(Order: 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5)*.
  2. Incompatibility restriction: Pronouns from the same block (e.g. *me* + *lui*, or *le* + *la*) CANNOT co-occur.
  3. Affirmative Imperative ordering (Post-verbal hyphenated):
     - Verb - *le / la / les* - *moi (m') / toi (t') / lui / nous / vous / leur* - *y* - *en* (*Donnez-le-moi !*, *Menez-y-nous !*).
  4. Negative Imperative ordering: Reverts strictly to the standard pre-verbal hierarchy (*Ne me le donnez pas !*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - Dual pronoun sequences: *me le*, *te la*, *le lui*, *les leur*, *l'y*, *nous en*, *lui en*, *y en*
  - Affirmative imperative hyphens: *-le-moi*, *-la-lui*, *-les-leur*

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il me montre son nouveau livre ; il me le prête pour le week-end. | Me enseña su nuevo libro; me lo presta para el fin de semana. / He shows me his new book; he lends it to me for the weekend. | Pre-verbal double pronoun (*me le*: Block 1 + Block 2) |
| Avez-vous envoyé la lettre au directeur ? Oui, je la lui ai envoyée hier. | ¿Ha enviado la carta al director? Sí, se la envié ayer. / Did you send the letter to the director? Yes, I sent it to him yesterday. | Pre-verbal double pronoun (*la lui*: Block 2 + Block 3) |
| Ce sont de très bonnes idées, donnez-nous-en plus ! | Son muy buenas ideas, ¡dans de ellas más! / These are very good ideas, give us more of them! | Affirmative imperative post-verbal order (*-nous-en*) |

### Topic ID: B1.3_RELATIVE_PRONOUNS_QUI_QUE_OU_DONT
- **Title:** Relative Pronouns (Qui, Que, Où, Dont)
- **Category:** Pronouns
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Link independent clauses and replace repetitive antecedents using simple relative pronouns based on syntactic function (*qui* = subject, *que* = direct object, *où* = place/time, *dont* = complement with *de*).
- **Prerequisites:** A2.3_RELATIVE_PRONOUNS
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/relative-pronouns/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. *Qui*: Replaces the subject (person or thing) of the relative clause. Never elides before a vowel (*L'homme qui parle...*).
  2. *Que / Qu'*: Replaces the direct object (COD) of the relative clause. Elides to *qu'* before a vowel or mute *h* (*Le livre qu'il lit...*).
  3. *Où*: Replaces a complement of place (where) or time (when) (*La ville où j'habite...*, *Le jour où nous sommes arrivés...*).
  4. *Dont*: Replaces any noun or phrase preceded by *de* (verbs with *de* like *parler de*, *avoir besoin de*, *être fier de*, or possessive *de*-phrases) (*Le projet dont tu parles...*, *La femme dont le fils est médecin...*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *qui* (who / which / that - subject)
  - *que / qu'* (whom / which / that - object)
  - *où* (where / when - location/time)
  - *dont* (of which / whose / about which - *de* complement)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| J'ai rencontré une femme qui parle cinq langues couramment. | Conocí a una mujer que habla cinco idiomas con fluidez. / I met a woman who speaks five languages fluently. | Relative pronoun *qui* as relative clause subject |
| C'est le film intéressant que nous avons vu le mois dernier. | Es la película interesante que vimos el mes pasado. / This is the interesting movie that we saw last month. | Relative pronoun *que* as COD + participle agreement (*vu*) |
| Voici la maison dont les fenêtres donnent sur le parc. | Aquí está la casa cuyas ventanas dan al parque. / Here is the house whose windows look out on the park. | Relative pronoun *dont* expressing possession (*dont les fenêtres*) |

  - *en* + feminine country/continent (*en France*, *en Europe*)

- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/top-100-verbs/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Standard Causative Meaning (*Faire* + Infinitive):** Expresses that the subject causes an action to occur or orders/arranges for someone else to perform an action, rather than performing it directly (*Je fais réparer ma voiture* = I am having my car fixed).
  2. **Reflexive Causative (*Se Faire* + Infinitive):** Used when the subject causes or undergoes an action done to themselves, whether intentionally (e.g., *Je me fais couper les cheveux*) or accidentally/unintentionally (*Il s'est fait voler son portefeuille*). In past tenses (*passé composé*), the past participle *fait* is **invariable** (*elle s'est fait construire une maison*).
  3. **Word Order & Pronoun Placement:** Object pronouns (direct, indirect, reflexive, or adverbial) precede the conjugated auxiliary or conjugated form of *faire*, NEVER the secondary infinitive (*Je le fais réparer*, *Il se la fait livrer*).
  4. **Agent Introduction (*Par* vs *À*):**
     - When the secondary infinitive has no direct object, the agent performing the action follows directly (*Je fais travailler les élèves*).
     - When the secondary infinitive takes a direct object, the agent is introduced by *par* or *à* (*Je fais laver la voiture par mon fils* / *Je fais lire ce livre à mes étudiants*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *faire* + infinitive (e.g., *faire réparer*, *faire construire*, *faire laver*, *faire cuire*)
  - *se faire* + infinitive (e.g., *se faire couper*, *se faire remarquer*, *se faire voler*)
  - Agent prepositions: *par*, *à*

|---|---|---|

| Elle se fait couper les cheveux dans ce nouveau salon chaque mois. | Ella se hace cortar el pelo en este nuevo salón cada mes. / She gets her hair cut in this new salon every month. | Reflexive causative (*se faire* + infinitive) with pre-verbal reflexive pronoun |
| Mon père le lui a fait comprendre sans s'énerver. | Mi padre se lo hizo entender sin enfadarse. / My father made him/her understand it without getting angry. | Double object pronoun placement before conjugated *faire* (*le lui a fait comprendre*) |

### Topic ID: B1.4_SEQUENCE_OF_TENSES
- **Title:** Concordance and Sequence of Tenses in Subordinate Clauses (La Concordance des Temps)
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Apply the correct tense shifts (sequence of tenses / concordance des temps) in subordinate clauses based on the tense of the main reporting clause across direct/indirect speech and complex sentence structures.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.1_PASSE_COMPOSE_VS_IMPARFAIT, B1.2_FUTURE_SIMPLE, B1.2_CONDITIONAL_PRESENT
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/direct-vs-indirect-speech/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Main Clause in Present / Future (*Présent*, *Futur simple*, *Futur proche*):
     - Subordinate action simultaneous/habitual -> Subordinate in **Présent** (*Il dit qu'il vient*).
     - Subordinate action prior/completed -> Subordinate in **Passé composé** (*Il dit qu'il a fini*).
     - Subordinate action posterior -> Subordinate in **Futur simple / Futur proche** (*Il dit qu'il viendra*).
  2. Main Clause in Past (*Passé composé*, *Imparfait*, *Plus-que-parfait*, *Passé simple*):
     - Subordinate action simultaneous -> Shifts Present to **Imparfait** (*Il a dit qu'il venait*).
     - Subordinate action prior -> Shifts Passé composé to **Plus-que-parfait** (*Il a dit qu'il avait fini*).
     - Subordinate action posterior -> Shifts Future to **Conditionnel présent** (Future-in-the-past) (*Il a dit qu'il viendrait*).
  3. Immutable Subordinate Tenses: If the original direct statement is already in the *Imparfait*, *Plus-que-parfait*, or *Conditionnel*, its tense remains unchanged when transposed after a past main reporting verb.

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - Subordinating conjunctions: *que / qu'*, *si*, *ce que*, *ce qui*, *lorsque*, *quand*
  - Present main reporting verbs: *dit*, *affirme*, *pense*, *déclare*, *sait*
  - Past main reporting verbs: *a dit*, *disait*, *a demandé*, *expliquait*, *a confirmé*

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Elle affirme qu'elle comprend la leçon et qu'elle réussira l'examen. | Afirma que entiende la lección y que aprobará el examen. / She asserts that she understands the lesson and will pass the exam. | Present main verb (*affirme*) + Present (*comprend*) / Future (*réussira*) subordinate |
| Le professeur a dit que les étudiants avaient préparé leurs devoirs. | El profesor dijo que los estudiantes habían preparado sus deberes. / The teacher said that the students had prepared their homework. | Past main verb (*a dit*) + Plus-que-parfait subordinate (*avaient préparé*) |
| Il me jurait qu'il viendrait dès qu'il aurait terminé son travail. | Me juraba que vendría en cuanto hubiera terminado su trabajo. / He swore to me that he would come as soon as he had finished his work. | Past main verb (*jurait*) + Conditionnel présent subordinate (*viendrait*) |

- **Linguistic Criteria:** Sentence containing a main clause finite verb followed by a subordinating element (*que*, *si*, *ce que*, *ce qui*) leading a subordinate clause finite verb matching tense sequence constraints (e.g. Past Main Verb + Imparfait / Plus-que-parfait / Conditionnel).

### Topic ID: B1.4_NEG_INFINITIF
- **Title:** Infinitive Negation & Word Order (Ne pas, Ne jamais, Ne plus + Infinitif)
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Form negated infinitive clauses correctly by placing negative adverbial pairs (*ne pas*, *ne jamais*, *ne plus*, *ne rien*) immediately before the infinitive verb, while positioning object and reflexive pronouns between the negative pair and the infinitive.
- **Prerequisites:** A1.2_NEGATION_BASICS, A2.2_INFINITIVE_CONSTRUCTIONS
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/infinitive-mood/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Bound Negative Pair Placement**: Unlike conjugated verbs where the verb separates *ne* and *pas* (*je ne fume pas*), when negating an infinitive verb, both elements of the negation (*ne pas*, *ne jamais*, *ne plus*, *ne rien*) are placed together directly BEFORE the infinitive verb (*ne pas fumer*, *ne jamais mentir*, *pour ne plus attendre*).
  2. **Pronoun Placement in Negated Infinitives**: Object pronouns (direct, indirect, reflexive, *y*, *en*) are placed AFTER the complete negative adverb pair (*ne pas*, *ne jamais*, etc.) and BEFORE the infinitive verb: `ne pas / ne jamais / ne plus + [PRONOUN(S)] + [INFINITIVE]` (e.g., *de ne pas lui parler*, *pour ne pas s'inquiéter*, *de ne jamais le lui dire*).
  3. **Exceptional Negation Structures with Infinitive**: With *personne* and *aucun(e)*, *ne* precedes the infinitive (or controlling verb/pronoun) while *personne* / *aucun(e)* follows the infinitive (*ne voir personne*, *n'en connaître aucun*).
  4. **Prepositional Complement Linking**: When the infinitive clause is introduced by a preposition (*de*, *pour*, *sans*, *à*), the preposition precedes the negative pair: `PREP + ne pas + [PRON] + INFINITIVE` (*afin de ne pas faire d'erreur*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *ne pas* + infinitif (not to...)
  - *ne jamais* + infinitif (never to...)
  - *ne plus* + infinitif (no longer to...)
  - *ne rien* + infinitif (nothing to... / to do nothing)
  - *afin de ne pas / pour ne pas / de ne pas* (in order not to / to not)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il m'a dit de ne pas arriver avant vingt et une heures. | Me dijo que no llegara antes de las nueve de la noche. / He told me not to arrive before 9:00 p.m. | Preposition *de* + *ne pas* + infinitive (*de ne pas arriver*) |
| Elle a promis de ne jamais lui mentir. | Prometió no mentirle nunca. / She promised never to lie to him/her. | *de ne jamais* + object pronoun *lui* + infinitive (*de ne jamais lui mentir*) |
| Il est important de ne pas s'inquiéter sans raison. | Es importante no preocuparse sin motivo. / It is important not to worry without reason. | *de ne pas* + reflexive pronoun *s'* + infinitive (*de ne pas s'inquiéter*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `ADV` with lemma *ne* followed by negative particle `ADV` (*pas*, *jamais*, *plus*, *rien*), optional sequence of object/reflexive `PRON` tokens, and an infinitive verb (`VERB` or `AUX` with `VerbForm=Inf`).
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    {"POS": "ADV", "LEMMA": "ne"},
    {"POS": "ADV", "LEMMA": {"IN": ["pas", "plus", "jamais", "rien", "point"]}},
    {"OP": "*"},
    {"POS": "VERB", "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["VerbForm=Inf"]}}
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:**
  - *Disambiguation against conjugated verb negation*: In conjugated clauses (*Je ne veux pas parler*), *ne* and *pas* envelope the conjugated modal/auxiliary verb (*veux*), so *pas* precedes the infinitive but *ne* precedes the conjugated verb. The pattern explicitly requires `ADV(ne)` to be placed directly adjacent to `ADV(pas|jamais|plus)`.
  - *Disambiguation of "ne ... personne / aucun"*: *personne* and *aucun* follow the infinitive (*de ne voir personne*). If targeting *ne ... personne*, an alternative pattern sequence placing `PRON(personne)` after `VERB(VerbForm=Inf)` must be handled as a separate rule.

### Topic ID: B1.4_SUBJ_INTRO
- **Title:** Introduction to the Subjunctive Mood (Le Subjonctif Présent)
- **Category:** Moods
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Understand the fundamental mood distinction between indicative objective reality and subjunctive subjective reality, master the 5 core trigger categories, and apply regular stem formation rules (-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent) as the gateway to B2 advanced subjunctive structures.
- **Prerequisites:** A1.2_PRESENT_TENSE_REGULAR, B1.2_CONDITIONNEL_PRESENT
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/subjunctive/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Indicative vs. Subjunctive Distinction: The indicative mood states objective facts, certainty, and external events (*Je sais qu'il vient*). The subjunctive mood expresses subjective reality, possibility, necessity, emotion, doubt, and volition in dependent subclause structures (*Je veux qu'il vienne*).
  2. Syntactic Two-Subject Requirement: Subjunctive constructions require a main clause with a trigger expression, the subordinator *que/qu'*, and a change of subject between the main clause and dependent clause ($S_1 \neq S_2$). If $S_1 = S_2$, an infinitive construction must be used instead (*Je veux partir* vs *Je veux qu'il parte*).
  3. The 5 Core Trigger Categories:
     - **Necessity:** Impersonal expressions of necessity (*il faut que*, *il est nécessaire que*).
     - **Desire / Volition:** Expressions of wishing, wanting, and order (*vouloir que*, *désirer que*, *demander que*, *souhaiter que*).
     - **Emotion:** Psychological states and feelings (*être content que*, *être triste que*, *avoir peur que*, *regretter que*).
     - **Doubt / Uncertainty:** Lack of certainty or disbelief (*douter que*, *il est douteux que*, *ne pas croire que*).
     - **Impersonal Evaluation:** Value judgments on actions (*il est important que*, *il est bon que*, *il est dommage que*).
  4. Basic Regular Formation Stem Rules:
     - Derive the primary stem from the 3rd person plural (*ils/elles*) present indicative by dropping *-ent* (e.g., *parl-*, *finiss-*, *vend-*).
     - Standard Subjunctive Endings across all three verb groups (-er, -ir, -re): *-e*, *-es*, *-e*, *-ions*, *-iez*, *-ent*.
     - Dual-stem verbs maintain present indicative 1P stem for *nous/vous* forms (*que nous buvions*, *qu'ils boivent*).
  5. Gateway Role to B2 Topics: Establishes the baseline morphological endings and initial 5 semantic trigger categories; serves as prerequisite for irregular subjunctive stems, conjunction triggers (*bien que*, *avant que*), and past subjunctive (*subjonctif passé*).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *il faut que* (it is necessary that)
  - *vouloir que* (to want that)
  - *être content(e) que* (to be happy that)
  - *douter que* (to doubt that)
  - *il est important que* (it is important that)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il faut que tu étudies cette leçon avant l'examen. | Es necesario que estudies esta lección antes del examen. / It is necessary that you study this lesson before the exam. | Impersonal necessity trigger (*il faut que*) + Subjunctive verb (*étudies*) |
| Le professeur veut que nous finissions le devoir ce soir. | El profesor quiere que terminemos la tarea esta noche. / The teacher wants us to finish the homework tonight. | Desire trigger (*veut que*) + 2nd group stem (*finiss-*) + ending (*-ions*) |
| Je suis ravit qu'elle comprenne la différence entre ces modes. | Me alegro de que ella entienda la diferencia entre estos modos. / I am delighted that she understands the difference between these moods. | Emotion trigger (*être ravi que*) + 3rd group stem (*comprenn-*) + ending (*-e*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Clause sequence featuring a recognized trigger verb/expression followed by subordinator *que/qu'* and a verb carrying finite subjunctive mood inflection (`Mood=Sub`).

  - *Indicative Declaratives:* Verbs of thinking and declaration (*croire*, *penser*, *dire*) take the indicative in affirmative sentences (*Je pense qu'il est là*), but trigger subjunctive when negated or inverted (*Je ne pense pas qu'il soit là*). ### Topic ID: B1.4_IMPERSONAL_EXPRESSIONS
- **Title:** Impersonal Expressions: General Infinitive (de + inf) vs Specific Subjunctive (que + subjonctif)
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Distinguish and master impersonal expressions (*il est possible*, *il vaut mieux*, *il est important*, *il s'agit de*, *il convient de*) contrasting general statements using *de* + infinitive against specific-subject subclauses using *que* + subjunctive.
- **Prerequisites:** B1.4_SUBJ_INTRO, A2.2_INFINITIVE_CONSTRUCTIONS
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/cest-vs-il-est/, https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/subjunctive/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **General Statement Rule (`de` + Infinitive):** When an impersonal expression makes a generic, non-subject-specific statement or recommendation, it takes `de` (or `d'`) followed by an infinitive verb (`il est + ADJ + de + INF`, `il convient de + INF`, `il s'agit de + INF`).
     - Example: *Il est important de bien dormir.* (It is important to sleep well.)
     - Example: *Il vaut mieux partir tôt.* (*valoir mieux* omits *de* before infinitive: *il vaut mieux + INF*).
     - Example: *Il s'agit de faire un effort.* (It is a matter of making an effort.)
  2. **Specific Subject Subclause Rule (`que` + Subjunctive):** When an impersonal expression introduces a clause targeting a specific agent/subject ($S_2$), it takes `que` (or `qu'`) followed by a subordinate clause with a finite verb in the Subjunctive mood (`il est + ADJ + que + S2 + SUBJUNCTIVE`).
     - Example: *Il est important que tu dormes bien.* (It is important that you sleep well.)
     - Example: *Il vaut mieux qu'elle vienne demain.* (It is better that she come tomorrow.)
  3. **Fixed Impersonal Idioms:**
     - *Il s'agit de* + noun / infinitive: Fixed impersonal pronominal structure meaning "it is a matter of / it concerns". Cannot take *que* + subjunctive directly in standard usage; requires *de* + infinitive or noun phrase.
     - *Il convient de* + infinitive vs *Il convient que* + subjunctive: Formal impersonal expression meaning "it is advisable / appropriate to".
     - *Il vaut mieux* + infinitive (no *de*) vs *Il vaut mieux que* + subjunctive: Impersonal comparative idiom ("it is better to / that").

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *il est possible de / que* (it is possible to / that)
  - *il vaut mieux [inf] / qu'il [subj]* (it is better to / that)
  - *il est important de / que* (it is important to / that)
  - *il s'agit de* (it is a question of / a matter of)
  - *il convient de / que* (it is appropriate to / that)

### Topic ID: B1.4_GERONDIF
- **Title:** The French Gerund (Le Gérondif: En + Participe Présent) & Adjectival Participle Distinction
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Form the French gerund (`en` + present participle stem `-ant`), master its adverbial functions (simultaneity, manner/means, condition, cause), enforce the strict subject co-referentiality constraint, and distinguish the invariant verbal gerund/participle from the variable verbal adjective (*adjectif verbal*).
- **Prerequisites:** A1.2_PRESENT_TENSE_REGULAR, A2.1_VERB_CONJUGATION
- **Source:** https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/ing-in-french/

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Formation:** The present participle (*participe présent*) is formed by dropping the *-ons* ending from the 1st person plural (*nous*) present indicative stem and adding *-ant* (e.g., *nous parlons* -> *parlant*, *nous finissons* -> *finissant*, *nous buvons* -> *buvant*). The **gérondif** is formed by preceding this present participle with the preposition **en** (*en parlant*, *en finissant*, *en buvant*).
     - **Irregular Stems (3 Exceptions):** *être* -> *en étant*, *avoir* -> *en ayant*, *savoir* -> *en sachant*.
  2. **Adverbial Uses of the Gerund:**
     - **Simultaneity / Concomitance:** Expresses two actions performed at the same time ("while doing"): *Il écoute de la musique en étudiant.* (Intensified with *tout*: *tout en étudiant*).
     - **Manner / Means / Instrument:** Explains how or by what means an action is achieved ("by doing"): *J'ai appris le français en lisant des livres.*
     - **Condition / Hypothesis:** Replaces a conditional *si* clause ("if / by doing"): *En travaillant régulièrement, tu réussiras.*
     - **Cause / Reason:** Replaces a causal conjunction like *parce que* ("by / since doing"): *En refusant d'obéir, il s'est mis en danger.*
  3. **Subject Co-referentiality Constraint:** The action of the gérondif MUST share the exact same subject as the main conjugated verb of the clause. 
     - Correct: *En sortant de la maison, Paul a vu un chat.* (Paul came out and Paul saw).
     - Incorrect (Dangling Participle): **En sortant de la maison, le chat a griffé Paul.* (Grammatically implies the cat left the house).
  4. **Gerund / Present Participle vs. Verbal Adjective Distinction:**
     - **Gérondif / Participe Présent:** Expresses a verbal action; completely **INVARIANT** in gender and number (*Une femme chantant en travaillant*).
     - **Adjectif Verbal:** Expresses a state or quality; agrees in gender and number with the noun it modifies (*Des histoires amusantes*, *Une personne fatigante*). Note spelling shifts in some roots (*provocant* verb vs *provocateur* / *fatiguant* verb vs *fatigant* adj).

- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:**
  - *en* + [participe présent en -ant] (while / by / upon -ing)
  - *tout en* + [participe présent] (while at the same time / even while)
  - *en train de* + infinitif (distinction: ongoing continuous state vs adverbial manner gerund)

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| En apprenant la liste de verbes, j'ai compris que le français est très compliqué. | Aprendiendo la lista de verbos, comprendí que el francés es muy complicado. / While learning the list of verbs, I understood that French is very complicated. | Gerund of simultaneity (*en apprenant*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** Preposition `ADP(en)` followed directly (or separated by object/reflexive pronouns or adverbs) by a verb in present participle form (`VERB` or `AUX` with `Tense=Pres|VerbForm=Part`).
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern = [
    {"LEMMA": "en"},
    {"POS": "VERB", "MORPH": {"IS_SUPERSET": ["VerbForm=Part", "Tense=Pres"]}}
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:**
  - *Disambiguation of "en" Prepositional Noun Syntagms:* `ADP(en)` is frequently followed by nouns (`ADP(en)` + `NOUN(France/voiture)`). Requiring the downstream token to have POS `VERB`/`AUX` and morphology `VerbForm=Part` excludes noun phrases (*en France*, *en train*, *en colère*).
  - *Pronominal & Negated Gerunds:* Handle clitic pronouns positioned between `en` and the participle (*en se lavant*, *en lui parlant*, *en ne disant rien*). The matcher includes `PRON` with zero-or-more operator (`"OP": "*"`).
  - *Verbal Adjective vs. Participle Agreement Disambiguation:* Present participles and gerunds never take gender/number inflections (*en chantant*, *des femmes chantant*). If a token ending in *-ant* exhibits gender or number agreement (`Gender=Fem` or `Number=Plur`), spaCy tags it as `ADJ` (*des paroles rassurantes*). The pattern restricts matching strictly to `POS=VERB/AUX`.
