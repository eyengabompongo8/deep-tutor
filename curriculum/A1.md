## Module A1.1: Foundations

### Topic ID: A1.1_NOUN_GENDER
- **Title:** Gender of Nouns & Plural Endings
- **Category:** Grammar
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Identify grammatical gender (masculine/feminine) from noun endings and form regular (-s) and irregular (-x, -aux) plurals.
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/noun-gender-rules/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. All French nouns possess grammatical gender (masculine or feminine).
  2. Common masculine noun suffixes: `-age` (*le fromage*), `-ment` (*le gouvernement*), `-eau` (*le bateau*), `-fon` (*le téléphone*), `-isme` (*le réalisme*), `-ing` (*le parking*).
  3. Common feminine noun suffixes: `-tion`/`-sion` (*la nation*, *la télévision*), `-té` (*la liberté*), `-ette` (*la bicyclette*), `-ence`/`-ance` (*la différence*, *la france*), `-ure` (*la voiture*).
  4. Plural Rule 1: Add `-s` to singular nouns (*un livre* -> *des livres*, *une chaise* -> *des chaises*).
  5. Plural Rule 2: Nouns ending in `-eau`, `-eu`, or `-au` take `-x` (*un château* -> *des châteaux*, *un jeu* -> *des jeux*).
  6. Plural Rule 3: Nouns ending in `-al` change to `-aux` (*un journal* -> *des journaux*, *un animal* -> *des animaux*).
  7. Plural Rule 4: Singular nouns ending in `-s`, `-x`, or `-z` remain unchanged (*un bus* -> *des bus*, *une voix* -> *des voix*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *le, la, l', les, un, une, des, -s, -x, -aux*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Le garçon lit un livre à la bibliothèque. | El niño lee un libro en la biblioteca. / The boy reads a book at the library. | Masculine singular noun (*garçon*, *livre*) & feminine (*bibliothèque*) |
| La voiture rouge traverse la grande ville. | El coche rojo cruza la gran ciudad. / The red car crosses the big city. | Feminine singular noun ending in -ure (*voiture*) |
| Les enfants regardent de magnifiques bateaux sur le lac. | Los niños miran barcos magníficos en el lago. / The children look at magnificent boats on the lake. | Plural noun ending in -eau taking -x (*bateaux*) |
| Mon frère lit deux journaux tous les matins. | Mi hermano lee dos periódicos todas las mañanas. / My brother reads two newspapers every morning. | Plural noun ending in -al changing to -aux (*journaux*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: NOUN`, checking morph features `Gender=Masc`, `Gender=Fem`, `Number=Sing`, `Number=Plur`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern = [
    {"POS": {"IN": ["NOUN", "PROPN"]}}
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Distinguish homonyms with gender shift (*le livre* = book vs *la livre* = pound sterling/half kilo; *le tour* = turn/trick vs *la tour* = tower). Check determiner agreement for disambiguation.

### Topic ID: A1.1_ARTICLES
- **Title:** Definite, Indefinite, and Partitive Articles
- **Category:** Grammar
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Correctly use definite (*le, la, l', les*), indefinite (*un, une, des*), and partitive (*du, de la, de l'*) articles based on countability and specific vs. non-specific context.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/articles/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Definite Articles (*le, la, l', les*): Used for specific, known nouns or general concepts/tastes (*j'aime le chocolat*). *le* and *la* elide to *l'* before vowels or silent *h* (*l'eau*, *l'homme*).
  2. Indefinite Articles (*un, une, des*): Used for unspecific countable nouns (*un stylo*, *une pomme*, *des amis*).
  3. Partitive Articles (*du, de la, de l'*): Used for uncountable quantities, mass nouns, food, and abstract qualities (*je bois du café*, *elle a de la chance*). Formed by preposition *de* + definite article (*de + le = du*, *de + les = des*).
  4. Contracted Articles: Preposition *à* + *le* = *au*, *à* + *les* = *aux*. Preposition *de* + *le* = *du*, *de* + *les* = *des*.
  5. Article Drop after Negation: Indefinite (*un, une, des*) and partitive (*du, de la, de l'*) articles turn into *de / d'* in simple negative sentences (*je n'ai pas de pain*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *le, la, l', les, un, une, des, du, de la, de l', au, aux*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Le professeur explique la leçon aux étudiants. | El profesor explica la lección a los estudiantes. / The teacher explains the lesson to the students. | Definite articles (*le*, *la*) and contracted article (*aux*) |
| Marie achète un livre et une pomme au marché. | Marie compra un libro y una manzana en el mercado. / Marie buys a book and an apple at the market. | Indefinite articles (*un*, *une*) with countable nouns |
| Le matin, je bois du café avec du lait et de l'eau. | Por la mañana, bebo café con leche y agua. / In the morning, I drink coffee with milk and water. | Partitive articles (*du*, *de l'*) with mass nouns |
| Nous n'avons pas de sucre dans la cuisine. | No tenemos azúcar en la cocina. / We do not have any sugar in the kitchen. | Article mutation to *de* in negative sentence |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: DET` attached to `HEAD POS: NOUN` via `dep: det`. Morphological features `PronType=Art`, `Definite=Def` / `Ind`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern = [
    {
        "POS": "DET",
        "LEMMA": {"IN": ["le", "un", "du", "de"]},
        "MORPH": {"REGEX": "PronType=(Art|Prs)"},
    },
    {"POS": "NOUN"},
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Distinguish *du* as partitive article vs *du* as prepositions contraction (*de + le* indicating possession/origin: *la clé du bureau*). Check dependency tree (`nmod` vs `det`).

### Topic ID: A1.1_PRONOUNS
- **Title:** Subject Pronouns & Formality (Tu vs. Vous)
- **Category:** Pronouns
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Select appropriate French personal subject pronouns (*je, tu, il, elle, on, nous, vous, ils, elles*) and apply the sociolinguistic distinction between informal *tu* and formal/plural *vous*.
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/subject-pronouns/`, `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/tu-vs-vous/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. French subject pronouns replace noun subjects and determine verb conjugation.
  2. 1st Person: *je / j'* (singular, elides before vowel/silent h), *nous* (plural).
  3. 2nd Person: *tu* (singular informal: used with peers, children, family, close friends), *vous* (formal singular or any plural).
  4. 3rd Person Singular: *il* (masculine singular / impersonal subject), *elle* (feminine singular), *on* (informal "we" / indefinite "one", takes 3rd person singular verb conjugation).
  5. 3rd Person Plural: *ils* (masculine plural or mixed gender group), *elles* (strictly all-feminine plural).
  6. *Tu* vs *Vous* Rule: Use *tu* (tutoiement) when addressing 1 person informally. Use *vous* (vouvoiement) for polite/professional single addressee or multiple people.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *je, j', tu, il, elle, on, nous, vous, ils, elles*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| J'habite à Paris et j'étudie la langue française. | Vivo en París y estudio la lengua francesa. / I live in Paris and I study the French language. | 1st person singular pronoun elision (*J'*) |
| Tu parles espagnol avec tes collègues? | ¿Hablas español con tus colegas? / Do you speak Spanish with your colleagues? | 2nd person singular informal subject pronoun (*Tu*) |
| Comment vous vous appelez, Monsieur Dupont? | ¿Cómo se llama usted, Señor Dupont? / What is your name, Mr. Dupont? | 2nd person singular formal subject pronoun (*Vous*) |
| On va au cinéma ce soir avec nos amis. | Vamos al cine esta noche con nuestros amigos. / We are going to the cinema tonight with our friends. | Informal 1st person plural subject pronoun (*On*) with 3rd person singular verb |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: PRON`, `PronType=Prs`, `Person=1|2|3`, `DEP: nsubj` or `nsubj:expl`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
patterns = [
    [{"POS": {"IN": ["PRON", "ADP"]}, "LEMMA": {"IN": ["je", "tu", "il", "elle", "on", "nous", "vous", "ils", "elles", "j'", "tu"]}}],
    [{"POS": "PRON"}],
    [{"TEXT": {"REGEX": "(?i)^(je|tu|il|elle|on|nous|vous|ils|elles|j'|on)$"}}]
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** *Il* used in impersonal expressions (*il faut*, *il pleut*) has `DEP: nsubj:expl`. *Nous* and *vous* can act as direct/indirect object pronouns or disjunctive pronouns (*avec nous*) - verify `DEP` tag is `nsubj`.

### Topic ID: A1.1_CEST_ILEST
- **Title:** Presentative Expressions (C'est / Ce sont vs. Il est / Elle est)
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Distinguish presentative construction *C'est / Ce sont* (introducing modified nouns/names) from adjectival description *Il est / Elle est* (unmodified adjectives/professions).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`, `A1.1_PRONOUNS`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/cest-vs-il-est/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. *C'est* (plural: *Ce sont*): Used to identify or introduce a person/thing followed by a modified noun with article/possessive (*c'est un étudiant*, *ce sont mes amis*), proper names (*c'est Pierre*), demonstrative pronouns (*c'est ça*), or neutral adjectives referring to general situations (*c'est facile*, *c'est beau*).
  2. *Il est / Elle est* (plural: *Ils sont / Elles sont*): Used to describe specific people/objects followed directly by an unmodified adjective (*il est intelligent*, *elle est grande*), an unarticulated profession/nationality (*il est médecin*, *elle est française*), or time expressions (*il est trois heures*).
  3. Rule of Thumb: If an article (*un, une, le, la, mon*) precedes the noun, use *C'est / Ce sont*. If no article is present before a profession or nationality, use *Il/Elle est*.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *c'est, ce sont, il est, elle est, ils sont, elles sont*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| C'est un grand médecin français. | Es un gran médico francés. / He is a great French doctor. | *C'est* + article + modified noun |
| Il est médecin dans un hôpital à Lyon. | Es médico en un hospital en Lyon. / He is a doctor in a hospital in Lyon. | *Il est* + unarticulated profession |
| Ce sont les clés de ma voiture. | Son las llaves de mi coche. / These are the keys to my car. | *Ce sont* + plural noun with article |
| Le français, c'est facile et intéressant! | El francés, ¡es fácil e interesante! / French, it is easy and interesting! | *C'est* + neutral adjectival expression |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `LEMMA: ce` or `PRON` + `AUX: être` followed by `DET`+`NOUN` vs `ADJ`/`NOUN`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
patterns = [
    [{"LEMMA": "ce"}, {"OP": "*"}, {"LEMMA": "être"}],
    [{"LEMMA": "il"}, {"OP": "*"}, {"LEMMA": "être"}]
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** *C'est* can elide (*c'*). *Ce sont* handles plural subjects. Make sure parser handles *c'est + ADJ* (e.g. *c'est vrai*) where *c'est* refers to a clause/situation.

### Topic ID: A1.1_ADJECTIVES
- **Title:** Descriptive Adjectives & BAGS Position Rules
- **Category:** Grammar
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Apply gender and number agreement to descriptive adjectives and place them correctly before or after the noun using the BAGS rule.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/adjectives/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Gender Agreement: Feminine adjectives usually add `-e` to masculine form (*grand* -> *grande*, *petit* -> *petite*). If masculine ends in silent `-e`, no change occurs (*jeune* -> *jeune*). Irregular feminine forms: *blanc/blanche*, *beau/belle*, *nouveau/nouvelle*, *vieux/vieille*, *bon/bonne*.
  2. Number Agreement: Plural adjectives usually add `-s` (*grands*, *grandes*). Masculine ending in `-al` often takes `-aux` (*national* -> *nationaux*). Masculine ending in `-s` or `-x` does not change (*gris* -> *gris*, *heureux* -> *heureux*).
  3. Position Rule (BAGS): Most French adjectives follow the noun (*un livre intéressant*, *une voiture bleue*). However, BAGS adjectives precede the noun:
     - **B**eauty: *beau/joli*
     - **A**ge: *jeune/vieux/nouveau*
     - **G**oodness: *bon/mauvais*
     - **S**ize: *grand/petit/gros/long*
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *beau, belle, nouveau, nouvelle, vieux, vieille, grand, petit, bon, mauvais, joli*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Pierre lit un livre intéressant dans la chambre. | Pierre lee un libro interesante en la habitación. / Pierre reads an interesting book in the room. | Post-posed descriptive adjective (*intéressant*) |
| C'est une belle maison avec un petit jardin. | Es una casa bonita con un jardín pequeño. / It is a beautiful house with a small garden. | Pre-posed BAGS adjectives (*belle*, *petit*) |
| Marie porte des robes bleues et élégantes. | Marie lleva vestidos azules y elegantes. / Marie wears blue and elegant dresses. | Plural feminine adjective agreement (*bleues*, *élégantes*) |
| Nous avons acheté un nouveau téléphone portable. | Hemos comprado un nuevo teléfono móvil. / We bought a new mobile phone. | Irregular masculine BAGS adjective before vowel/consonant (*nouveau*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: ADJ`, modifying `NOUN` via `dep: amod`. Morphological agreement matching `Gender` and `Number` of head `NOUN`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern = [
    {"POS": "DET", "OP": "?"},
    {"POS": "ADJ", "OP": "*"},  # Pre-posed BAGS adjectives
    {"POS": "NOUN"},
    {"POS": "ADJ", "OP": "*"},  # Post-posed regular adjectives
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Watch for *beau*, *nouveau*, *vieux* changing to *bel*, *nouvel*, *vieil* before masculine singular nouns starting with a vowel or silent h (*un bel homme*, *un nouvel appartement*).

### Topic ID: A1.1_POSSESSIVE
- **Title:** Possessive Adjectives
- **Category:** Pronouns / Determiners
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Express possession using possessive determiners matching the gender and number of the possessed item, not the possessor.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/possessive-adjectives/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Agreement Rule: Possessive adjectives agree in gender and number with the noun being possessed (the item), NOT the possessor.
  2. 1st Person Singular (my): *mon* (masc sing), *ma* (fem sing), *mes* (plural).
  3. 2nd Person Singular (your informal): *ton* (masc sing), *ta* (fem sing), *tes* (plural).
  4. 3rd Person Singular (his/her/its): *son* (masc sing), *sa* (fem sing), *ses* (plural).
  5. 1st Person Plural (our): *notre* (masc/fem sing), *nos* (plural).
  6. 2nd Person Plural / Formal (your): *votre* (masc/fem sing), *vos* (plural).
  7. 3rd Person Plural (their): *leur* (masc/fem sing), *leurs* (plural).
  8. Special Euphonic Rule: Before a feminine singular noun starting with a vowel or silent *h*, use *mon, ton, son* instead of *ma, ta, sa* (*mon amie*, *son histoire*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *mon, ma, mes, ton, ta, tes, son, sa, ses, notre, nos, votre, vos, leur, leurs*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Paul cherche son sac et ses clés dans la maison. | Paul busca su bolsa y sus llaves en la casa. / Paul looks for his bag and his keys in the house. | 3rd person singular possessive (*son*, *ses*) |
| J'écris une lettre à mon amie française. | Escribo una carta a mi amiga francesa. / I write a letter to my French female friend. | Euphonic rule (*mon* before feminine vowel *amie*) |
| Vous apportez votre ordinateur et vos documents. | Usted trae su ordenador y sus documentos. / You bring your computer and your documents. | Formal 2nd person possessive (*votre*, *vos*) |
| Les enfants font leurs devoirs dans leur chambre. | Los niños hacen sus deberes en su habitación. / The children do their homework in their room. | 3rd person plural possessive (*leurs*, *leur*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: DET`, `Poss=Yes` or `PronType=Prs`, attached to `NOUN` head.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern = [
    {
        "POS": "DET",
        "LEMMA": {
            "IN": ["mon", "ton", "son", "notre", "votre", "leur"]
        },
        "MORPH": {"REGEX": "Poss=Yes"},
    },
    {"POS": "NOUN"},
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** *Leur* can be a possessive determiner (*leur voiture*, *leurs voitures*) or an indirect object pronoun (*je leur parle*). Possessive determiner precedes a noun (`POS: DET`), while object pronoun precedes a verb (`POS: PRON`).

## Module A1.2: Present Tense & Core Verbs

### Topic ID: A1.2_ETRE_AVOIR
- **Title:** Essential Auxiliaries: Être & Avoir in Present Tense & Expressions
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Conjugate the fundamental irregular verbs *être* (to be) and *avoir* (to have) in the present indicative and apply them in core idiomatic expressions (age, hunger, thirst, feelings).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/etre-avoir/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. *Être* Conjugation (Present Indicative): *je suis*, *tu es*, *il/elle/on est*, *nous sommes*, *vous êtes*, *ils/elles sont*.
  2. *Avoir* Conjugation (Present Indicative): *j'ai*, *tu as*, *il/elle/on a*, *nous avons*, *vous avez*, *ils/elles ont*.
  3. Idiomatic Uses of *Avoir* (expressing state/needs where English/Spanish uses "to be" / "tener"):
     - Age: *J'ai 25 ans* (I am 25 years old / Tengo 25 años).
     - Hunger/Thirst: *avoir faim*, *avoir soif*.
     - Heat/Cold: *avoir chaud*, *avoir froid*.
     - Fear/Need: *avoir peur*, *avoir besoin de*.
  4. Idiomatic Uses of *Être*: Location (*être à Paris*), nationality/profession (*être canadien*), temporary or permanent state (*être fatigué*, *être grand*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont, ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont, faim, soif, chaud, froid, peur, besoin, ans*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Je suis étudiant et je suis très content de participer. | Soy estudiante y estoy muy contento de participar. / I am a student and I am very happy to participate. | Present indicative of *être* (identity & state) |
| Nous avons trente ans et nous avons deux enfants. | Tenemos treinta años y tenemos dos hijos. / We are thirty years old and we have two children. | Present indicative of *avoir* (age & possession) |
| Après le sport, les enfants ont très soif et faim. | Después del deporte, los niños tienen mucha sed y hambre. / After sports, the children are very thirsty and hungry. | Idiomatic expressions with *avoir* (*avoir soif/faim*) |
| Vous êtes à Paris pour les vacances cette semaine? | ¿Está usted en París para las vacaciones esta semana? / Are you in Paris for the holidays this week? | *Être* indicating location/presence |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `LEMMA: être` or `LEMMA: avoir`, `POS: AUX` or `VERB`, `MORPH: Tense=Pres|VerbForm=Fin`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python
pattern_etre = [
    {"POS": "PRON", "OP": "?"},
    {"LEMMA": "être", "MORPH": {"REGEX": "Tense=Pres"}},
]

pattern_avoir = [
    {"POS": "PRON", "OP": "?"},
    {"LEMMA": "avoir", "MORPH": {"REGEX": "Tense=Pres"}},
    {"POS": {"IN": ["NOUN", "ADJ"]}, "OP": "?"},
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:** Distinguish *ils sont* (3rd pl *être*) from *ils ont* (3rd pl *avoir*) which sound identical in non-liaison oral environments. In text parser, check exact token string.

### Topic ID: A1.2_ER_VERBS
- **Title:** Regular First Group (-ER) Verbs & Stem Mutations
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Conjugate regular 1st group verbs ending in `-er` in the present indicative and handle orthographic stem mutations for `-ger` and `-cer` verbs.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/regular-er-verbs/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Regular `-er` verbs comprise ~90% of French verbs (e.g., *parler, habiter, aimer, travailler, étudier, regarder, écouter*).
  2. Present Indicative Endings: Infinitive stem + `-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent`.
     - *parler* stem `parl-`: *je parle, tu parles, il parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils parlent*.
  3. Note: The endings `-e, -es, -ent` are silent in pronunciation.
  4. Orthographic Stem Mutations:
     - `-ger` verbs (*manger, voyager*): add an `-e-` before `-ons` in *nous* form to preserve soft /ʒ/ sound (*nous mangeons*).
     - `-cer` verbs (*commencer, lancer*): change `c` to `ç` before `-ons` in *nous* form to preserve soft /s/ sound (*nous commençons*).
     - `-eler` / `-eter` verbs (*appeler, jeter*): double the consonant before silent endings (*j'appelle*, *tu jettes*, but *nous appelons*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *parle, parles, parlons, parlez, parlent, mangeons, commençons, appelle*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Nous parlons français avec notre professeur tous les jours. | Hablamos francés con nuestro profesor todos los días. / We speak French with our teacher every day. | Regular -ER verb (*parler*) 1st person plural |
| Elle habite à Lyon et travaille dans une banque. | Ella vive en Lyon y trabaja en un banco. / She lives in Lyon and works in a bank. | 3rd person singular -ER verbs (*habiter*, *travailler*) |

### Topic ID: A1.2_IR_VERBS
- **Title:** Regular Second Group (-IR) Verbs (Finir Type)
- **Category:** Verbs
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Conjugate regular 2nd group verbs ending in `-ir` characterized by the `-iss-` plural stem extension in the present indicative.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`, `A1.2_ER_VERBS`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/regular-ir-verbs/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. 2nd Group `-ir` verbs (e.g., *finir, choisir, grandir, réussir, réfléchir, réagir, obéir*) follow a strict regular pattern.
  2. Present Indicative Endings:
     - Singular Stem: Infinitive minus `-ir` + `-is, -is, -it` (*je finis, tu finis, il finit*).
     - Plural Stem: Infinitive minus `-ir` + `-issons, -issez, -issent` (*nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent*).

  4. Non-Group 2 `-ir` verbs: Verbs like *partir, sortir, venir, courir* belong to the 3rd group (irregular) and do NOT take `-iss-`.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *finis, finit, finissons, finissez, finissent, choisis, choisit, choisissons, réussissons*.

- **Category:** Syntax

- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/sentence-structure/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Basic Canonical Sentence Order: Subject + Verb + Direct/Indirect Object (SVO). (e.g., *Pierre mangé une pomme*).
  2. Adverb Placement in Simple Tenses: Short high-frequency adverbs (*toujours, souvent, beaucoup, bien, mal, déjà, encore*) follow the conjugated verb immediately (*je parle souvent français*, *il mange bien*).

### Topic ID: A1.3_NEGATION
- **Title:** Basic Negation (Ne ... pas / N' ... pas) & Article Mutation
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Form negative sentences using the bipartite wrapper *ne ... pas* / *n' ... pas* and apply indefinite/partitive article mutation (*de / d'*).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_ARTICLES`, `A1.2_ER_VERBS`, `A1.3_SENTENCE_STRUCTURE`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/negation/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Bipartite Negation: The negative particle *ne* precedes the conjugated verb and *pas* follows it directly: `Subject + ne + Verb + pas + Object`.
  2. Elision: *ne* elides to *n'* before a verb beginning with a vowel or silent *h* (*je n'aime pas*, *il n'habite pas*).
  3. Article Mutation Rule: In negative sentences, indefinite articles (*un, une, des*) and partitive articles (*du, de la, de l'*) change to *de* (or *d'* before a vowel):
     - Affirmative: *J'ai un chien.* -> Negative: *Je n'ai pas de chien.*
     - Affirmative: *Je bois du lait.* -> Negative: *Je ne bois pas de lait.*
  4. Exception to Mutation: Article mutation does NOT occur with the verb *être*:
     - *C'est un problème.* -> *Ce n'est pas un problème.*
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *ne, n', pas, de, d'*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Je ne parle pas espagnol mais je parle français. | No hablo español pero hablo francés. / I do not speak Spanish but I speak French. | Standard negation around verb (*ne ... pas*) |
| Il n'a pas de voiture et il n'a pas d'argent. | No tiene coche y no tiene dinero. / He does not have a car and he does not have money. | Negation with elision (*n'*) and article mutation (*de*, *d'*) |
| Nous ne mangeons pas de viande pendant le carême. | No comemos carne durante la cuaresma. / We do not eat meat during Lent. | Partitive article mutation (*du* -> *de*) |
| Ce n'est pas une bonne idée de sortir sous la pluie. | No es una buena idea salir bajo la lluvia. / It is not a good idea to go out in the rain. | Exception to article mutation with *être* (*pas une*) |

### Topic ID: A1.3_QUESTIONS
- **Title:** Interrogative Sentences & Question Words
- **Category:** Syntax
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Form questions using intonation, *Est-ce que*, pronoun inversion, and interrogative words (*qui, que, où, quand, pourquoi, comment, combien*).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`, `A1.2_ER_VERBS`, `A1.3_SENTENCE_STRUCTURE`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Three Structural Levels of Yes/No Question Formation:
     - **Intonation (Informal):** Declarative sentence + rising intonation + `?` (*Tu parles français?*).
     - **Est-ce que (Standard):** *Est-ce que* + declarative sentence + `?` (*Est-ce que tu parles français?*). Elides to *Est-ce qu'* before vowel (*Est-ce qu'il vient?*).
     - **Inversion (Formal):** Verb - Subject Pronoun + `?` (*Parles-tu français?*). If 3rd person verb ends in a vowel, insert euphonic `-t-` (*Parle-t-il français?*).
  2. Question Words (Information Questions):
     - *Qui* (Who): *Qui est là?*
     - *Que / Qu'est-ce que* (What): *Que faites-vous?* / *Qu'est-ce que tu fais?*
     - *Où* (Where): *Où habitez-vous?*
     - *Quand* (When): *Quand partez-vous?*
     - *Pourquoi* (Why): *Pourquoi étudies-tu le français?* (Answer with *Parce que*).
     - *Comment* (How): *Comment vous vous appelez?*
     - *Combien de* (How much/many): *Combien de frères as-tu?*
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *est-ce que, est-ce qu', qui, que, quoi, où, quand, pourquoi, comment, combien, parce que, ?*.

## Module A1.4: Foundational Vocabulary Grammar

### Topic ID: A1.4_TELLING_TIME
- **Title:** Telling Time in French
- **Category:** Grammar / Vocabulary
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Ask for and express time using *Il est... heure(s)*, fixed expressions for quarters (*et quart*, *moins le quart*), half-hours (*et demie* / *et demi*), noon/midnight (*midi*, *minuit*), exact hours (*pile*), and time of day indicators (*du matin*, *de l'après-midi*, *du soir*).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`, `A1.2_ETRE_AVOIR`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/telling-time/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Asking for Time:** Standard/formal: *Quelle heure est-il ?*; informal: *Il est quelle heure ?*; polite: *Vous avez l'heure ?*.
  2. **Core Presentative Construction:** Always begins with impersonal pronoun *il* + verb *être* in 3rd person singular (*Il est...*).
  3. **Expressing Hours (*heure / heures*):**
     - Singular (1 o'clock): *Il est une heure.* (*une* is feminine singular matching feminine noun *heure*).
     - Plural (2-11 o'clock): *Il est deux heures*, *Il est trois heures*, etc. (noun *heures* takes plural `-s`).
     - Unlike English ("It is two o'clock" -> "It is two"), the word *heure(s)* must never be dropped in standard French.
  4. **Fixed Noon / Midnight Expressions:**
     - 12:00 PM: *Il est midi.* (No *heures* is added).
     - 12:00 AM: *Il est minuit.* (No *heures* is added).
  5. **Minutes and Quarters (12-Hour Spoken Clock):**
     - Minutes past (1-29): Add minute cardinal number directly after *heure(s)* (*Il est deux heures dix* = 2:10).
     - Quarter past (:15): Add *et quart* (*Il est quatre heures et quart* = 4:15).
     - Half past (:30): Add *et demie* with feminine `-e` after *heures* (*Il est cinq heures et demie* = 5:30), but masculine *et demi* after *midi* / *minuit* (*midi et demi*, *minuit et demi*).
     - Minutes to (31-59): Subtract remaining minutes from the next hour using *moins* (*Il est six heures moins dix* = 5:50).
     - Quarter to (:45): Use *moins le quart* (*Il est sept heures moins le quart* = 6:45).
     - Sharp / Exact hour: Add *pile* (*Il est huit heures pile* = 8:00 sharp).
  6. **Time of Day Clarification (12-Hour Clock):**
     - Morning (AM): *du matin* (*huit heures du matin* = 8:00 AM).
     - Afternoon (PM): *de l'après-midi* (*trois heures de l'après-midi* = 3:00 PM).
     - Evening/Night (PM): *du soir* (*neuf heures du soir* = 9:00 PM).
  7. **24-Hour Official Clock:**
     - Used in schedules, timetables, and formal speech (e.g., *quatorze heures trente*, *dix-neuf heures quarante-cinq*).
     - In 24-hour format, *et quart*, *et demie*, and *moins le quart* are NOT used; exact numbers are stated.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *quelle heure, il est, heure, heures, midi, minuit, et quart, et demie, et demi, moins le quart, pile, du matin, de l'après-midi, du soir*.

|---|---|---|
| Quelle heure est-il s'il vous plaît? | ¿Qué hora es, por favor? / What time is it, please? | Interrogative structure asking for time |

| Il est midi et demi et nous allons déjeuner. | Es mediodía y medio y vamos a almorzar. / It is half past twelve (noon) and we are going to lunch. | Fixed noon expression (*midi*) + masculine *et demi* |
| Il est huit heures moins le quart du soir. | Son las ocho menos cuarto de la noche. / It is a quarter to eight in the evening. | Plural hours (*huit heures*) + *moins le quart* + *du soir* |
| Le train part à dix-neuf heures pile. | El tren sale a las diecinueve horas en punto. / The train leaves at 19:00 sharp. | 24-hour clock structure + exact hour indicator (*pile*) |

  2. Disambiguate *il est* as time presentative vs adjectival description (*il est grand*) or modal expression (*il est nécessaire*). Time expressions require a downstream temporal noun lemma (`heure`, `midi`, `minuit`).
  3. Ensure *quart* in *moins le quart* is parsed with determiner `le`, whereas *et quart* has no determiner (*et quart*, NOT *et le quart*).

### Topic ID: A1.4_PREPOSITIONS_PLACE
- **Title:** Basic Prepositions of Place (Location & Spatial Position)
- **Category:** Grammar / Vocabulary
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Express physical position, spatial relationships, and location using simple prepositions (*dans, sur, sous, devant, derrière, entre*) and compound prepositional locutions (*à côté de, en face de, près de*), including mandatory article contraction rules (*de + le = du*, *de + les = des*).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/prepositions/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Simple Place Prepositions:** Single-word prepositions place a noun in physical space:
     - *dans* (in / inside): Enclosed 3D space, container, or geographic area (*dans le sac*, *dans la chambre*, *dans la ville*).
     - *sur* (on / upon): On top of a surface (*sur la table*, *sur le mur*).
     - *sous* (under / beneath): Directly underneath an object (*sous le lit*, *sous la table*).
     - *devant* (in front of): Positioned at the front of something (*devant la maison*).
     - *derrière* (behind): Positioned at the back of something (*derrière l'immeuble*).
     - *entre* (between): Located in the space separating two landmarks or within a group (*entre la banque et la poste*, *entre les deux arbres*).
  2. **Compound Prepositional Locutions (Multi-Word Prepositions ending in *de*):**
     - *à côté de* (next to / beside): Adjacent spatial placement (*à côté de la banque*).
     - *en face de* (opposite / facing): Vis-à-vis placement across an open space or street (*en face du musée*).
     - *près de* (near / close to): General spatial proximity (*près de la gare*).
  3. **Mandatory Article Contractions with *de*:**
     - Prepositional locutions ending in *de* contract when followed by definite articles *le* or *les*:
       - *de + le* $\rightarrow$ **du** (*près du parc*, *à côté du cinéma*, *en face du restaurant*).
       - *de + les* $\rightarrow$ **des** (*à côté des magasins*, *près des arbres*).
       - *de + la* remains **de la** (*près de la banque*).
       - *de + l'* remains **de l'** before vowels/silent *h* (*en face de l'hôpital*).
  4. **Spatial vs. Temporal Distinction:**
     - *devant* is strictly spatial ("in front of"). Do not confuse with temporal *avant* ("before in time").
     - *dans* can express future time frame (*dans deux jours*), but in spatial context it requires a physical container or location noun complement.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *dans, sur, sous, devant, derrière, entre, à côté de, en face de, près de, du, des*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Le chat dort sur la table et le chien est sous le lit. | El gato duerme sobre la mesa y el perro está debajo de la cama. / The cat sleeps on the table and the dog is under the bed. | Simple prepositions of spatial position (*sur*, *sous*) |
| Il y a un grand arbre devant la maison et un jardin derrière. | Hay un gran árbol delante de la casa y un jardín detrás. / There is a big tree in front of the house and a garden behind. | Spatial prepositions of relative position (*devant*, *derrière*) |
| La boulangerie se trouve à côté du supermarché et près de la gare. | La panadería se encuentra al lado del supermercado y cerca de la estación. / The bakery is located next to the supermarket and near the station. | Compound prepositions with contraction (*à côté du*, *près de*) |
| Le restaurant est situé en face du musée, entre la banque et la poste. | El restaurante está situado enfrente del museo, entre el banco y la oficina de correos. / The restaurant is located opposite the museum, between the bank and the post office. | Compound preposition (*en face du*) and dual-landmark preposition (*entre*) |

### Topic ID: A1.4_STRESSED_PRONOUNS
- **Title:** Tonic / Stressed Pronouns (Disjunctive Pronouns)
- **Category:** Pronouns
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Master the form and syntax of tonic/stressed pronouns (*moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles, soi*) used after prepositions, for emphasis, after *c'est / ce sont*, in comparisons, with intensifiers, and in standalone expressions.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_PRONOUNS`, `A1.1_CEST_ILEST`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/stressed-pronouns/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Pronoun Forms:** Stressed pronouns (pronom toniques/disjonctifs) correspond to personal subject pronouns:
     - 1st Sing (*je*) -> **moi**
     - 2nd Sing (*tu*) -> **toi**
     - 3rd Sing Masc (*il*) -> **lui**
     - 3rd Sing Fem (*elle*) -> **elle**
     - 3rd Sing Indefinite (*on*) -> **soi**
     - 1st Plur (*nous*) -> **nous**
     - 2nd Plur / Formal (*vous*) -> **vous**
     - 3rd Plur Masc (*ils*) -> **eux**
     - 3rd Plur Fem (*elles*) -> **elles**
  2. **After Prepositions:** Stressed pronouns MUST follow prepositions (e.g., *avec, pour, sans, chez, contre, de, à*). Example: *chez moi*, *avec lui*, *pour eux*. Subject pronouns (*je, tu, il, ils*, etc.) cannot follow a preposition (*avec il* is strictly ungrammatical).
  3. **For Emphasis (Subject Dislocation / Reduplication):** Placed at the beginning or end of a clause, set off by a comma, to emphasize the subject (*Moi, je préfère le café*; *Lui, il adore le chocolat*).
  4. **After Presentative Expressions (*C'est / Ce sont*):** Used directly after presentatives to identify specific people (*C'est moi*, *C'est lui qui conduit*, *Ce sont eux qui préparent le repas*).
  5. **Compound Subjects & Objects (Coordinated Pronouns):** Used when joining multiple nouns or pronouns with conjunctions (*et, ou, ni*). Example: *Ma sœur et moi*, *Lui et Sylvie*, *Ni toi ni lui*.
  6. **In Comparisons:** Used after *que* in comparative constructions (*Il est plus grand que moi*, *Elle est aussi rapide que lui*).
  7. **Standalone Responses & Short Expressions:** Function independently in single-word or short answers (*Qui vient ? - Moi !*) and idiomatic expressions (*moi aussi* = me too, *moi non plus* = me neither).
  8. **With Intensifier Suffix `-même(s)`:** Appended with `-même` (singular) or `-mêmes` (plural) for self-reflexive emphasis (*moi-même, toi-même, lui-même, elle-même, soi-même, nous-mêmes, vous-mêmes, eux-mêmes, elles-mêmes*).
  9. **Indefinite Reflexive Pronoun *soi*:** Refers back to indefinite/general subjects (*on, chacun, tout le monde, personne*). Example: *Chacun pour soi*; *Il faut avoir confiance en soi*.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles, soi, moi aussi, moi non plus, c'est moi, chez moi, avec toi, toi-même, soi-même*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Moi, je préfère habiter à Paris avec ma famille. | Yo, prefiero vivir en París con mi familia. / Me, I prefer living in Paris with my family. | Subject emphasis (*Moi, je*) + preposition with stressed pronoun (*avec ma famille*) |
| C'est lui qui prépare le dîner ce soir chez nous. | Es él quien prepara la cena esta noche en nuestra casa. / He is the one cooking dinner tonight at our place. | Presentative *C'est* + stressed pronoun (*lui*) + preposition (*chez nous*) |
| Ma sœur et moi allons au cinéma sans eux. | Mi hermana y yo vamos al cine sin ellos. / My sister and I are going to the cinema without them. | Compound subject (*Ma sœur et moi*) + preposition with stressed pronoun (*sans eux*) |
| Il est plus grand que toi, mais tu es aussi rapide que lui. | Él es más alto que tú, pero tú eres tan rápido como él. / He is taller than you, but you are as fast as him. | Stressed pronouns in comparative constructions (*que toi*, *que lui*) |
| Chacun doit penser à soi et faire le travail soi-même. | Cada uno debe pensar en sí mismo y hacer el trabajo uno mismo. / Everyone must think of oneself and do the work oneself. | Indefinite stressed pronoun (*soi*, *soi-même*) after preposition |

### Topic ID: A1.4_IL_Y_A
- **Title:** Existential Expression & Temporal Ago: Il y a
- **Category:** Grammar / Expressions
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Master the existential expression *il y a* (there is / there are), its negative form *il n'y a pas de*, and its temporal usage *il y a + [time]* (ago).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_ARTICLES`, `A1.2_ETRE_AVOIR`, `A1.3_NEGATION`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/french-word-of-the-day/il-y-a/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Existential Meaning ("There is / There are"):**
     - Impersonal construction consisting of subject pronoun *il*, adverbial pronoun *y*, and conjugated verb *avoir* in 3rd person singular present (*il y a*).
     - Invariable in number: Used for both singular and plural nouns (*il y a un livre*, *il y a des livres*).
     - Followed by indefinite articles (*un, une, des*), partitive articles (*du, de la, de l'*), numbers (*deux, trois*), or indefinite determiners (*plusieurs, beaucoup de*).
  2. **Negative Construction ("There is no / There are no"):**
     - Formed by wrapping negation around *y a*: *il n'y a pas de / d'*.
     - *ne* elides to *n'* before *y*: `Il + n' + y + a + pas + de/d' + noun`.
     - Article mutation rule applies: Indefinite (*un, une, des*) and partitive articles (*du, de la, de l'*) change to *de* (or *d'* before a vowel): *Il n'y a pas de voiture*, *Il n'y a pas d'eau*.
  3. **Temporal Duration / Ago ("Il y a + time period"):**
     - Placed before a time period to indicate elapsed time in the past (equivalent to English "ago" or Spanish "hace [tiempo]").
     - Structure: `[Passé composé / Present action] + il y a + [duration]` (e.g., *Je suis arrivé il y a trois jours* = I arrived three days ago).
  4. **Interrogative Forms:**
     - Standard with *est-ce que*: *Est-ce qu'il y a...?*
     - Inversion (formal): *Y a-t-il...?*
     - Informal intonation: *Il y a...?*
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *il y a, il n'y a pas, il n'y a pas de, il n'y a pas d', y a-t-il, est-ce qu'il y a*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il y a un grand parc en face de la gare. | Hay un gran parque frente a la estación. / There is a large park in front of the station. | Affirmative existential *il y a* + singular noun |
| Il y a beaucoup d'étudiants dans la salle de classe. | Hay muchos estudiantes en el aula. / There are many students in the classroom. | Affirmative existential *il y a* + plural quantity |
| Il n'y a pas de pain sur la table du petit-déjeuner. | No hay pan en la mesa del desayuno. / There is no bread on the breakfast table. | Negative *il n'y a pas de* + article mutation (*de*) |
| Il n'y a pas d'eau dans la bouteille. | No hay agua en la botella. / There is no water in the bottle. | Negative *il n'y a pas d'* with vowel elision (*d'*) |
| Je suis arrivé à Paris il y a trois ans. | Llegué a París hace tres años. / I arrived in Paris three years ago. | Temporal usage *il y a* + duration (*trois ans*) |

- **Linguistic Criteria:** `PRON` (il) + `PRON` (y) + `AUX/VERB` (a/avoir) + `DET`/`ADP` (un/une/des/de). Morphosyntactic structure for `il y a` and negative `il n'y a pas de`.

```python

- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:**
  1. Distinguish *il y a* as an existential predicate (`dep: expl` on *y*, `dep: nsubj:expl` on *il*) vs literal locative constructions where *y* refers to a previously mentioned place (*il y a trouvé son sac* = he found his bag there). In literal locative usage, *avoir* is an auxiliary for a past participle or transitive verb, whereas in existential *il y a*, *a* is the main verb taking a direct object / complement.
  2. Disambiguate temporal *il y a* from existential *il y a*: Temporal *il y a* is followed by a numerical time duration (`NUM` + temporal `NOUN` like *jours*, *mois*, *ans*) and functions syntactically as an adverbial modifier (`advmod` / `obl`) attached to a main clause verb.
  3. Spoken informal reduction: In spoken informal French, *il y a* often reduces to *y a* (*y a quelqu'un ?*), dropping the subject pronoun *il*. The matcher can set `"OP": "?"` on `PRON` (*il*) for informal register detection.

### Topic ID: A1.4_DEMONSTRATIVE_ADJ
- **Title:** Demonstrative Adjectives (Ce, Cet, Cette, Ces, -ci, -là)
- **Category:** Grammar / Determiners
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Select and apply the appropriate demonstrative adjective (*ce, cet, cette, ces*) based on noun gender, number, and phonetics (vowel/silent h), and use suffix particles (*-ci*, *-là*) for spatial/temporal distinction and contrast.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/demonstrative-adjectives/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Function:** Demonstrative adjectives (adjectifs démonstratifs) point out specific people, objects, or ideas ("this", "that", "these", "those"). They act as determiners preceding a noun.
  2. **Masculine Singular:**
     - **ce**: Used before masculine singular nouns starting with a consonant or aspirated *h* (*ce livre*, *ce garçon*, *ce hameau*).
     - **cet**: Used before masculine singular nouns starting with a vowel sound or silent *h* (*cet homme*, *cet arbre*, *cet appartement*). This phonetic adjustment avoids hiatus (vowel clash).
  3. **Feminine Singular:**
     - **cette**: Used before all feminine singular nouns, regardless of whether they start with a consonant or vowel (*cette femme*, *cette école*, *cette maison*, *cette histoire*).
  4. **Plural (Masculine & Feminine):**
     - **ces**: Used before all plural nouns of either gender (*ces livres*, *ces hommes*, *ces femmes*, *ces écoles*).
  5. **Disambiguating Proximity/Distance & Contrast with Suffix Particles (`-ci` / `-là`):**
     - French demonstrative determiners do not intrinsically distinguish between "this" and "that" or "these" and "those".
     - To specify proximity ("this/these") vs. distance ("that/those") or create explicit contrast, suffix particle **-ci** (here/near) or **-là** (there/far) is appended directly to the noun.
     - **-ci** indicates closeness in space or time ("this / these here"): *ce livre-ci* (this book here), *ces jours-ci* (these days).
     - **-là** indicates distance or previous mention ("that / those there"): *cette maison-là* (that house over there), *ce jour-là* (that day).
  6. **Demonstrative Adjective vs. Demonstrative Pronoun / Presentative:**
     - Demonstrative adjective (*ce / cet / cette / ces*) strictly modifies a noun (`POS: DET`, attached to `NOUN`).
     - Demonstrative pronoun / presentative (*c'est*, *ce sont*, *ce qui*, *ce que*, *celui-ci*) stands alone or precedes a verb/relative pronoun (`POS: PRON`).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *ce, cet, cette, ces, -ci, -là, ce livre-ci, cette maison-là, ces hommes*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Ce garçon lit un livre intéressant dans le parc. | Este niño lee un libro interesante en el parque. / This boy reads an interesting book in the park. | Masculine singular demonstrative before consonant (*ce*) |
| Cet homme habite dans cet appartement à Paris. | Este hombre vive en este apartamento en París. / This man lives in this apartment in Paris. | Masculine singular demonstrative before silent h and vowel (*cet*) |
| Cette femme travaille dans cette école depuis deux ans. | Esta mujer trabaja en esta escuela desde hace deux años. / This woman works in this school for two years. | Feminine singular demonstrative (*cette*) |
| Ces étudiants adorent étudier ces langues étrangères. | Estos estudiantes adoran estudiar estas lenguas extranjeras. / These students love studying these foreign languages. | Plural demonstrative for masculine and feminine nouns (*ces*) |
| Je préfère ce livre-ci mais ma sœur achète cette robe-là. | Prefiero este libro de aquí pero mi hermana compra aquel vestido de allá. / I prefer this book here but my sister buys that dress over there. | Proximity and distance suffix particles (*-ci*, *-là*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: DET`, `PronType=Dem` matching lemmas `["ce", "cet", "cette", "ces"]` attached to a head `NOUN` or `PROPN` via `dep: det`, optionally followed by suffix hyphenated particle `ci` or `là` (`dep: advmod` or `dep: fixed`).
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    {
        "POS": "DET",

### Topic ID: A1.4_DAYS_MONTHS_SEASONS
- **Title:** Days of the Week, Months, Seasons, and Date Expressions
- **Category:** Grammar / Vocabulary
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Correctly express days of the week, months, seasons, and full dates using appropriate temporal prepositions (*en*, *au*, *du...au*) and determiners (*le lundi* vs *lundi*).
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`, `A1.4_TELLING_TIME`
- **Source:** `https://www.lawlessfrench.com/grammar/dates/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Gender & Capitalization:** All days of the week (*lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche*), months of the year (*janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre*), and seasons (*printemps, été, automne, hiver*) are **masculine** and written in **lowercase** in French (unless placed at the very start of a sentence).
  2. **Days of the Week & Determiners:**
     - **Specific Single Occurrence (Bare Day):** No article is used when referring to a single specific upcoming or past day (*Je pars lundi* = I am leaving on Monday).
     - **Habitual / Recurring Action (*le* + Day):** Definite article *le* before a day name indicates a recurring action ("every [day]" / "on [days]"). E.g., *Le lundi, je travaille* (On Mondays / Every Monday, I work).
     - **Intervals (*du... au...*):** Expressing ranges from one day to another uses *du [day] au [day]* (e.g., *du lundi au vendredi* = from Monday to Friday).
  3. **Months & Temporal Prepositions:**
     - **Preposition *en*:** Used directly before months (*en janvier*, *en mai*, *en décembre* = in January, May, December).
     - **Expression *au mois de*:** Formal compound preposition alternative (*au mois de mai* = in the month of May).
  4. **Seasons & Temporal Prepositions:**
     - **Vowel / Silent H Starter (*en*):** Use *en* for seasons starting with a vowel sound (*en été*, *en automne*, *en hiver*).
     - **Consonant Starter (*au*):** Use *au* (contraction of *à + le*) for *printemps* (*au printemps*).
  5. **Expressing Full Dates:**
     - **Standard Formula:** Definite article *le* + cardinal number + month (+ year) (e.g., *le 14 juillet*, *le 24 janvier 2012*).
     - **1st of the Month Exception:** Uses the ordinal number *premier* / *1er* instead of cardinal *un* (*le 1er mai*, *le premier janvier*). All other days use cardinal numbers (*le 2 mai*, *le 15 août*).
     - **Date Presentatives:** State the current date using *C'est le...*, *Nous sommes le...*, or informal *On est le...* (e.g., *Nous sommes le 14 juillet*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche, janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre, printemps, été, automne, hiver, le, en, au, du, 1er, premier*.

## Module A1.4: Foundational Vocabulary Grammar

### Topic ID: A1.4_QUANTITY_EXPRESSIONS
- **Title:** Adverbs of Quantity & De Article Replacement Rule
- **Category:** Grammar / Vocabulary
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Express quantities using adverbs of quantity (beaucoup de, peu de, un peu de, assez de, trop de) and apply the rule where de/d' replaces partitive and indefinite articles before nouns.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_ARTICLES`, `A1.3_SENTENCE_STRUCTURE`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/grammar/adverbs-of-quantity/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. Quantity Adverb Structure: Quantity adverbs are linked to nouns via the fixed preposition *de* (or *d'* before vowels or silent *h*): `Adverb of Quantity + de / d' + Noun`.
  2. Core Quantity Adverbs:
     - *beaucoup de* (a lot of / many / much)
     - *peu de* (little / few - indicates lack or small quantity)
     - *un peu de* (a little / a bit of - indicates small positive quantity)
     - *assez de* (enough / sufficient)
     - *trop de* (too much / too many)
     - *plus de* (more of) / *moins de* (less / fewer of) / *combien de* (how much / how many)
  3. Article Replacement Rule: When an adverb of quantity quantifies a noun, it completely replaces indefinite (*un, une, des*) and partitive (*du, de la, de l'*) articles with fixed *de / d'*. Never combine quantity adverbs with partitive or indefinite articles (e.g., *beaucoup de pain*, NOT *beaucoup du pain*; *trop de livres*, NOT *trop des livres*).
  4. Elision Rule: The preposition *de* elides to *d'* when the following noun or modifier begins with a vowel or silent *h* (*beaucoup d'eau*, *assez d'heures*).
  5. Invariance of De: The preposition *de* remains invariant in form regardless of whether the target noun is a singular mass noun (*beaucoup d'eau*) or a plural countable noun (*beaucoup de livres*).
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *beaucoup de, peu de, un peu de, assez de, trop de, plus de, moins de, combien de, d'*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples
| French Sentence | ES/EN Translation | Targeted Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Il y a beaucoup d'étudiants dans la bibliothèque. | Hay muchos estudiantes en la biblioteca. / There are many students in the library. | Quantity adverb (*beaucoup d'*) with elision replacing plural article |
| Elle a peu de temps mais elle a un peu d'espoir. | Tiene poco tiempo pero tiene un poco de esperanza. / She has little time but she has a little hope. | Contrast between *peu de* (lack) and *un peu d'* (small amount) |
| Nous avons assez d'argent pour acheter ce livre. | Tenemos suficiente dinero para comprar este libro. / We have enough money to buy this book. | Quantity adverb (*assez d'*) with singular mass noun before vowel |
| Tu bois trop de café et tu manges trop de sucre. | Bebes demasiado café y comes demasiado azúcar. / You drink too much coffee and eat too much sugar. | Quantity adverb (*trop de*) replacing partitive article (*du*) |

#### spaCy Corpus Detection Strategy
- **Linguistic Criteria:** `POS: ADV` (quantity adverb) followed by `POS: ADP` (`LEMMA: de`) attached to `HEAD POS: NOUN` or `PROPN`.
- **spaCy Matcher Pattern:**
```python

    {"LEMMA": "un", "POS": "DET", "OP": "?"},
    {
        "POS": "ADV",
        "LEMMA": {
            "IN": [
                "beaucoup",
                "peu",
                "assez",
                "trop",
                "plus",
                "moins",
                "combien",
                "tant",
                "tellement",
            ]
        },
    },
    {"POS": "ADP", "LEMMA": "de"},
    {"POS": "ADJ", "OP": "*"},
    {"POS": {"IN": ["NOUN", "PROPN"]}},
]
```
- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:**
  1. **Adverb vs Modifier of Adjective/Verb:** Quantity adverbs like *beaucoup* or *trop* modifying a verb directly without a noun (*j'aime beaucoup*, *il travaille trop*) - disambiguated by strictly requiring `POS: ADP` (`LEMMA: de`) followed by `NOUN`/`PROPN`.
  2. **Specific Determiner Contraction (`de + les = des`):** When referring to a specific, known set of entities (*beaucoup des élèves de cette classe*), *de* contracts with *les* into *des*. In spaCy, *des* is tagged `DET`, failing the strict `ADP: de` pattern match as intended.
  3. **Idiomatic Exception (`bien des`):** The quantity expression *bien des* retains the article *des* (*bien des choses*), unlike standard quantity adverbs which enforce fixed *de*.
  4. **Elision Handling (`d'`):** spaCy normalizes token *d'* to `LEMMA: de`, enabling seamless matching for elided forms (*beaucoup d'eau*, *assez d'argent*).

### Topic ID: A1.4_NUMBERS_CARDINAL
- **Title:** Cardinal Numbers & Counting Rules (0-100, Hundreds, Thousands & Plural Rules)
- **Category:** Vocabulary / Numbers
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Master French cardinal numbers from 0 to millions, including compound number construction, hyphenation rules (traditional vs 1990 spelling rectifications), plural agreements for *cent* and *vingt*, and nominal usage with *de* for *million* and *milliard*.
- **Prerequisites:** `A1.1_NOUN_GENDER`, `A1.1_ARTICLES`
- **Source:** `https://www.frenchlearner.com/vocabulary/numbers/`

#### Grammatical Breakdown
- **Morphosyntactic Rules:**
  1. **Basic Numbers 0–16 & Tens:**
     - 0–16: *zéro, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix, onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize*.
     - Tens: *vingt* (20), *trente* (30), *quarante* (40), *cinquante* (50), *soixante* (60).
  2. **Vigesimal Counting System (70–99):**
     - 70–79: *soixante-dix, soixante-onze, soixante-douze...* (60 + 10, 60 + 11...).
     - 80–89: *quatre-vingts, quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-deux...* (4 x 20, 4 x 20 + 1...).
     - 90–99: *quatre-vingt-dix, quatre-vingt-onze, quatre-vingt-douze...* (4 x 20 + 10, 4 x 20 + 11...).
  3. **Hyphenation & Connection Rules (*et-un* / Hyphenation):**
     - **Traditional Spelling:** Hyphens link compound numbers under 100 except where *et* is used for numbers ending in 1 up to 71 (*vingt et un*, *trente et un*, *quarante et un*, *cinquante et un*, *soixante et un*, *soixante et onze*). Exception: *quatre-vingt-un* and *quatre-vingt-onze* take hyphens and no *et*.
     - **1990 Spelling Reform (Rectifications orthographiques):** Hyphens are used between ALL elements in compound numbers (*vingt-et-un*, *deux-cents*, *trois-mille-quatre-cents*). Both orthographies are standard.
  4. **Plural Rules for *Vingt* and *Cent*:**
     - *Vingt* takes a plural `-s` (*quatre-vingts*) ONLY when multiplied AND NOT followed by another number (*quatre-vingts* = 80 vs *quatre-vingt-trois* = 83).
     - *Cent* takes a plural `-s` (*deux cents*, *trois cents*) ONLY when multiplied AND NOT followed by another number (*deux cents* = 200 vs *deux cent cinq* = 205).
     - Note: In dates or ordinal positions (*page deux cent*, *l'an quatre-vingt*), *cent* and *vingt* remain singular.
  5. **Invariance of *Mille*:**
     - *Mille* (thousand) is strictly invariant and NEVER takes a plural `-s` (*trois mille*, *cinq mille*).
  6. **Large Numbers (*Million* & *Milliard*) and Preposition *De*:**
     - *Million* (million) and *milliard* (billion) are masculine nouns, NOT simple adjectives. They take a plural `-s` when multiplied (*deux millions*, *trois milliards*).
     - When directly preceding a noun, *million(s)* and *milliard(s)* MUST be followed by the preposition *de / d'* (*trois millions d'habitants*, *deux millions d'euros*). If followed by further numbers before the noun, *de* is used after the total phrase (*deux millions trois cent mille euros*).
  7. **Gender Agreement of *Un/Une*:**
     - *Un* changes to feminine *une* when modifying feminine nouns (*twenty-one girls* = *vingt et une filles*, *thirty-one pages* = *trente et une pages*). All other cardinal numbers are gender-invariant.
- **Key Trigger Words / Markers:** *zéro, un, une, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix, onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize, vingt, trente, quarante, cinquante, soixante, soixante-dix, quatre-vingts, quatre-vingt-dix, cent, cents, mille, million, millions, milliard, milliards*.

#### Canonical Sentence Examples

- **Detection Edge Cases & Disambiguation:**
  1. **Indefinite Article vs. Numeral (*un / une*):** In French spaCy models (`fr_core_news_sm`/`lg`), token *un/une* can be parsed as `DET` (indefinite article: *un livre*) or `NUM` (numeral: *j'en ai un*). Patterns matching exact number counts must check `POS: NUM` or `MORPH: NumType=Card`.

  4. **Hyphenated Compounds:** Spelled-out compound numbers like *quatre-vingt-dix-neuf* may be parsed as a single token or split tokens depending on tokenizer settings. Using `"OP": "+"` on `POS: NUM` handles multi-token compounds gracefully.
